**Recommendation:** Major revision. The paper’s pre-registration transparency is unusually strong, but several internal contradictions and unsupported mechanistic claims remain. It is not ready for a DOI deposit as-is. Target venue: an LLM interpretability / reproducibility workshop; after the fixes below it could be a good workshop paper, but it would not clear a main-conference bar in its current form.

## New findings

### 1. Plain-language pre-registration claim still contradicts the rank amendment
**Where:**  
“Pre-registration means we locked the entire analysis pipeline --- the metric, the residualization …, the bootstrap, and the pass bar --- before any data was collected, so that no post-hoc choices could be fit to the result.”

**What’s wrong:**  
This appears early, before the rank-trapdoor disclosure. But the paper later says the rank was *not* in the original seal and was fixed by amendment after an exploratory sweep. The “metric” was therefore not fully locked before data collection.

**Why it matters:**  
This is the first plain-language description of pre-registration a reader sees, and it overstates what was actually locked. It conflicts with the otherwise honest abstract/§3.2 disclosure.

**Fix:**  
Reword: “We locked the analysis plane, residualization, bootstrap, and pass bar before data collection; the rank was later fixed at 1 by a disclosed v3.1 amendment.”

### 2. Seed/provenance account is internally inconsistent
**Where:**  
“The development, preliminary and powered runs share a generator, prompt family and seed (20260423)” yet “at a given index the n=200 and n=600 runs agree on the contradiction label only 75% of the time and on chain length 50% of the time, so the same index is a different puzzle.”

**What’s wrong:**  
If the runs truly share the same generator, prompt family, and seed, the first 200 prompts in the n=200 and n=600 runs should be identical — 100% agreement, not 75%. The 75% agreement also contradicts the sentence immediately before it: `sample_id` is said to “therefore carry no content,” but 75% label agreement at matched indices is far above chance, so the index carries partial content.

**Why it matters:**  
This is the central evidence-provenance argument. The paper cannot currently explain why the same seed does not reproduce the same prompt sequence, and it simultaneously claims `sample_id` is content-free while showing it is informative.

**Fix:**  
State exactly how each run’s sequence was generated and why identical seed/generator settings did not yield identical first-200 prompts. If there is a shuffle or per-run RNG state, say so. Store content-bearing prompt IDs or hashes.

### 3. Pre-registered robustness check is specified but never reported
**Where:**  
Sealed spec: “Robustness check at \texttt{d\_F\_topk32}.”

**What’s wrong:**  
I could not find any E18 result using `d_F_topk32`. The E18 table and figure report only `d_F_lowrank32` residualization.

**Why it matters:**  
A pre-registered robustness check appears to be missing from the results. Reviewers and future auditors will notice this quickly.

**Fix:**  
Report the `d_F_topk32` E18 check alongside the primary `d_F_lowrank32` result, or explicitly state that it was not run and why.

### 4. “Residualization absorbs the correction” is asserted, not demonstrated
**Where:**  
“The sealed E18 verdict is unaffected by the correction (residualization against \texttt{d\_F\_lowrank32}, also computed in the buggy geometry, absorbs the coordinate mismatch).”

**What’s wrong:**  
The paper speaks of absorption as a mechanism, but the only evidence shown is that the pass/fail verdict survived. The same figure caption notes Qwen 2.5’s E18 AUROC moves from 0.7445 to 0.6687 — so the E18 magnitude was *not* absorbed. The bootstrap CI may also change, even if the verdict does not.

**Why it matters:**  
This is a stronger claim than the evidence supports. A reader may infer the residualised E18 metric is invariant to the coordinate error, which the paper itself refutes.

**Fix:**  
Softening: “Residualization was sufficient to preserve the E18 verdict in this instance, though point estimates and CIs still move.” Do not state that residualization absorbs the bias as a general property without showing it.

### 5. The “Simpson’s-paradox” sites are likely products of per-cell sign fitting
**Where:**  
“At sealed r=1: the pool says Raw decisive (Δ = −0.140), but the stratum-level reading is Fisher decisive at cl=2 (+0.065 …) and tied at cl=5 (+0.002 …). Mistral is never Raw-decisive at the stratum level — the pooled ‘Raw’ verdict is a mixing artifact of two chain-length subgroups whose Fisher and Raw discrimination axes have different orientations.”

**What’s wrong:**  
Under the disclosed orientation rule, every cell’s AUROC sign is fitted on that cell’s labels. Therefore `Δ_oriented` is not a common estimand across pooled and stratified cells: the sign of Fisher and Raw can flip between cells. A pooled negative Δ with positive stratum Δs can arise from orientation choice, not from a standard Simpson’s reversal of a fixed estimator.

**Why it matters:**  
The paper calls these “Simpson’s-paradox sites” and treats them as structural motifs. Readers may interpret them as substantive chain-length × rank interactions when part or all of the effect may be an artifact of the oriented metric.

**Fix:**  
Recompute the stratified comparison using a common orientation fixed in advance, or report unoriented AUCs for these cells. Otherwise relabel as “orientation-induced sign reversals” and explicitly warn the reader in the motif section, not only in the sealed-spec bullet.

### 6. §5.1 contains a near-circular mechanism claim
**Where:**  
“What does predict Fisher-vs-Raw is the discriminative strength of V_raw[0] itself.”

**What’s wrong:**  
Raw null ratio at rank 1 is the fraction of `Δh` outside `V_raw[0]`; its separation is therefore mechanically driven by the same `V_raw[0]` projection strength used as the explanatory variable. So “Raw wins when `V_raw[0]` discriminates” is close to restating “Raw wins when the Raw score discriminates.” This is not a fully independent mechanism.

**Why it matters:**  
The paper presents this as the load-bearing mechanistic finding, but as written it is partly tautological. The earlier commit-token predictor is explicitly shown *not* to work, leaving no independent explanation.

**Fix:**  
Reframe as a consistency check: “Raw success coincides with univariate `V_raw[0]` separation, as expected because the Raw score is based on that projection.” Then make the mechanistic claim only about *why* `V_raw[0]` separates for some architectures, using the token-level evidence, not about the predictive variable itself.

### 7. Baseline table’s “v3 sealed” is not the sealed E18 metric
**Where:**  
Baseline caption: “v3 sealed = \texttt{null\_ratio\_post\_rank1}.”  
Sealed E18 spec: “\texttt{null\_ratio\_post\_rank1} residualized against \texttt{d\_F\_lowrank32} via OLS.”

**What’s wrong:**  
The E18 sealed metric is residualised; the baseline table reports the un-residualised `null_ratio_post_rank1`. The same model, Qwen 2.5, appears as 0.6468 in the E18 table but 0.8967 in the baselines table, with no local note that the metrics differ.

**Why it matters:**  
A reader comparing the tables will see a large discrepancy and may suspect an error. Calling both “sealed” conflates two different metrics.

**Fix:**  
Rename the baseline column to “v3 (unresidualised)” or “v3 rank-1 null ratio” and add a sentence explaining that the sealed E18 metric is the residualised version.

### 8. Pipeline says first generated token is the answer token; mechanism table says otherwise for 3 of 6 models
**Where:**  
“The model is asked to output \texttt{Answer: YES} or \texttt{Answer: NO}; the gen\_step=1 token is the first token of the model's answer.”

Later, Table `tab:wu_axis` reports Mistral, Phi, and Gemma commit `\n` at 100%.

**What’s wrong:**  
For half the model lineup, the first generated token is a newline/formatting token, not the first token of the answer content. The pipeline description contradicts the paper’s own mechanism section.

**Why it matters:**  
It blurs the definition of the “commit moment” and may confuse readers about what `gen_step=1` is measuring.

**Fix:**  
Say “first generated token” or “commit token, which may be formatting/non-content for some models,” and cross-reference the newline-commit discussion.

### 9. N=100 stratified sample notation is mathematically wrong
**Where:**  
“We characterized both empirically on N=100 stratified puzzles per model (50 ctrl × 50 contr × 2 chain lengths, seed = 20260423).”

**What’s wrong:**  
As written, `50 ctrl × 50 contr × 2 chain lengths` implies 5000 puzzles. The intended design is presumably 25 per cell over the 2×2 factorial, i.e. 100 total.

**Why it matters:**  
It is a visible arithmetic typo in a paper whose main value is methodological precision.

**Fix:**  
Write “25 per cell × 2 contradiction levels × 2 chain lengths” or “50 ctrl and 50 contr, split across two chain lengths.”

### 10. E18 still says ~33% CI tightening is “consistent with” √3 narrowing
**Where:**  
“Bootstrap CIs are ∼33% tighter than the n=200 prelim (run-02), consistent with √3 narrowing for 3× more data.”

Later E17b text correctly says:  
“the √3 scaling implied by a 3× sample-size increase would give 42%, so the observed tightening falls slightly short of the independent-sample ideal.”

**What’s wrong:**  
33% is not consistent with 42%; the two sections disagree. This is a leftover of the earlier CI-narrowing issue and remains internally inconsistent.

**Why it matters:**  
A careful reader will notice the contradiction between E18 and E17b.

**Fix:**  
Align E18 wording with E17b: the observed tightening is smaller than the √3 ideal.

### 11. “No supervision target” conflicts with label-fitted sign
**Where:**  
“What separates PRI from that line is … an unsupervised geometric property of the commit step, with no fitted direction and no supervision target.”

Later:  
“For E17b the polarity is fitted on evaluation labels.”

**What’s wrong:**  
The v3 metric may be constructed without supervision, but the reported oriented AUROCs use signs fitted on evaluation labels. “No supervision target” is too strong as stated.

**Why it matters:**  
This is exactly the kind of overclaim that could embarrass the authors in public, especially given how carefully the paper elsewhere discloses sign fitting.

**Fix:**  
Clarify: “The geometric score is unsupervised, but the evaluation orientation/sign is fitted on labels; deployment requires an out-of-sample sign.”

## Single most valuable remaining change

Fix the **run-generation/provenance account** in §3.2. The current text both says the runs share a generator/prompt family/seed and reports only 75% contradiction agreement at matched indices, which cannot both be true as stated. That section is central to the paper’s credibility about what is and is not independent. The authors should either provide a precise, consistent explanation of how the runs differ despite the same seed, or store and report content-bearing prompt IDs.
