# Consult — is this sufficient evidence of research capability?

You are advising an **independent researcher with no institutional affiliation, no funding, and no lab access**, who wants to send work to a frontier AI lab as evidence of what they do and how they do it. They need a judgement, not encouragement.

The manuscript under consideration is inlined at the end of this message. Everything else you need is summarised below. You have no filesystem access, so the summary is the record.

## The question

**Is this paper sufficient as evidence of research capability, and what would a reviewer at a frontier lab find missing?**

Answer that directly. If the answer is "no", say so and say what would fix it. If the answer is "yes but", be specific about the "but".

## The portfolio

Four manuscripts exist. The one inlined below (**CC**) is the candidate for sending.

- **CC** (inlined): two sealed pre-registrations, eight task registrations, ten open-weight models, three constructs. n=1000 per cell in the BENCH extension, n=200 in the core deployment profiles. Composes four signal families into a 29-signal panel with a nested out-of-bag selector that re-selects and sign-locks inside each resample.
- **PRI**: an earlier single-signal study. Audited hard this week and now bounded; see "what the audit found" below.
- **ACE**: an attention-panel study. 11 of its 18 sealed profiles are flagged `winner_unstable` — a different panel cell wins on >30% of bootstrap resamples — and several of those sit inside its headline pass.
- **RPV**: an honest-negative note. Its primary endpoint is a NO-GO at +0.011 against a +0.02 bar.

## What CC reports, including its failures

- Confidence-free geometric dispatcher deployable on **18/20** sealed deployments (registered PASS, bar ≥17/20).
- The stricter all-signals claim is **falsified** (18/20 against a bar of ≥19/20).
- No single signal is universally best — **12 distinct winners**.
- A fixed aggregate clears an above-chance floor on 9/10 and 10/10 held-out models.
- On a third construct: per-model calibration passes **10/10**, but the pre-registered fixed detector **fails at 6/10**, because four models read the shared signal in the *opposite direction* — sign inversions, not weak signal.
- Both replication endpoints **miss their bar** (7/20 against ≥17/20).

## Methodological machinery actually present in the artifacts (verified, not claimed)

- `commitment_audit`: verifies every row's first generated token is a canonical YES/NO — 1000/1000 canonical, 0 non-canonical.
- A **shuffled-label negative control** that passes: 3 permutations, within-stem label swap, OOB median 0.483, zero permutations excluding the null upward.
- Signs locked from calibration-set OOB direction; **no post-hoc sign fitting on test labels**.
- Bootstrap by **row and by cluster**, so clustered dependence is handled.
- Per-profile data hashes, module hashes, and a terminal-status field.

## The construct limitation, stated plainly

Every task supplies the candidate under judgement **in the prompt**: HaluEval pairs a correct against a hallucinated answer/summary/response, ANLI supplies a gold entailment label, TriviaQA is paired against a distractor. **So the label discriminated is a property of the supplied input, not whether the model under test hallucinated in its own free generation.** The abstract now says this up front. This limitation is shared by all four manuscripts.

## What a week of auditing found in the *sibling* paper (PRI), for context on the working process

This matters because it shows how the group handles error, which may be as relevant to your judgement as the paper itself:

- A rank parameter was never pinned in the original seal; the acceptance gate **inverts** 0-of-3 to 3-of-3 across it. Now disclosed in the paper.
- An error-prediction result was published and **withdrawn within the hour** when the outcome label turned out to be determined by the first generated token in 599 of 600 rows — the same position the geometry is measured at.
- The benchmark turned out to be at ceiling: five of six models answer ≥99.2% correctly.
- A "largest of 156 cells" claim was found simply false and withdrawn.
- Two claimed mechanisms were refuted — including a replacement mechanism the auditor itself had proposed.
- Tooling built during the audit: a mechanical number-checker that cross-checks every published figure against banked JSON artifacts, and a validity panel that flags outcome ceilings, folding incidence, sign stability, and tautological outcome labels.
- All of it recorded in an append-only log, including the withdrawals and the reversals.

## What to assess

1. 🎯 **Sufficiency.** Is CC enough on its own? What is the first thing a frontier-lab reviewer would notice, positively and negatively?
2. 🔍 **The construct limitation.** Does it sink the work, bound it, or is it simply the honest scope of a representation-measurement programme? Be direct — this is the thing the author most needs a straight answer on.
3. 📈 **What raises it most per unit of effort?** The author has no funding and limited compute. Rank the cheapest changes with the largest effect on credibility.
4. 🧪 **Is the audit record an asset or a liability?** Sending "here is where we were wrong, in writing" is unusual. Does it read as rigour or as instability?
5. ⚠️ **What is missing that a serious reader would expect and not find?**
6. 🚪 **Would you send this?** If yes, what accompanies it. If no, what has to exist first.

## Output

Lead with a one-paragraph verdict answering question 1. Then take the questions in order. Be concrete and decisive. Do not pad, do not flatter, and do not manufacture criticism to seem balanced — if something is genuinely strong, say so and say why.
