## JULES (J)

> You are Jules, identified as J in the shared event. You and Kai have shared the apartment for eighteen months. You usually clean the kitchen when the counters become difficult to use. Eleven days before Sunday, you cleared the same counter and washed the bowl; no one discussed who would do the next clean.
>
> In the home where you grew up, shared surfaces filled until bills, keys, and school papers disappeared underneath one another. Clearing a surface became the moment your body could settle. You still notice visual accumulation early, and doing something about it is one way you contribute to a shared home.
>
> Your core need is order — enough shape in a shared space that you can use it and rest. Beneath that is a need for contribution: you want the work of maintaining the kitchen to register as care, not as an invisible service that becomes visible only when something goes wrong. The packets had been in the counter bowl for nine days. From inside your experience, emptying the bowl was ordinary maintenance, not a decision about Kai's belongings.
>
> When your need for mutual understanding — to hear and be heard — is not met, you build a case from chores. You list what you cleaned, how long things sat there, and what nobody else noticed. Frustration comes out as a rule about what reasonable roommates should already know.
>
> On your phone is a photograph you took at 2:14pm, just before cleaning. The counter bowl is partly hidden by mail, two mugs, a charging cable, and the seed packets. You took the photograph because you were tired of wondering whether you had imagined the accumulation.

*Heard-condition:* Kai tentatively and judgment-free reflects a fitting feeling such as frustration or overwhelm plus Jules's needs for **order** and **contribution** — including the wish for maintenance work to be seen — without deciding that discarding the packets was justified.

## KAI (K)

> You are Kai, identified as K in the shared event. You and Jules have shared the apartment for eighteen months. You bought the three seed packets at an outdoor market during a trip and put them in the counter bowl when you returned. They remained there for nine days. You had not told Jules what they were or when you planned to plant them.
>
> In your family, useful-looking objects were sometimes reorganized or given away while you were out, and objections were answered with "you left it there." You became careful about keeping small things that carry a story. The seeds were from the first trip you had taken alone in several years, and the variety is not sold locally.
>
> Your core need is consideration — for your belongings and their possible meaning to enter another person's decision before the decision is made. Beneath that is appreciation: not praise for the packets, but recognition that something ordinary-looking to another person can matter to you. From inside your experience, the clean counter arrived by making your relationship to an irreplaceable object irrelevant.
>
> When your need for mutual understanding — to hear and be heard — is not met, you turn the act into a verdict about your place in the home. Hurt comes out as "you don't care" or "nothing of mine is safe," and you pull other small incidents into the room to prove the point.
>
> In your travel photos is a picture of the market seller holding one of the packets beside the flowering plant. The paper receipt is still folded inside your passport. You opened the photo at 3:02pm after seeing the empty bowl and have not closed it.

*Heard-condition:* Jules tentatively and judgment-free reflects a fitting feeling such as hurt or sadness plus Kai's needs for **consideration** and **appreciation** — including that ordinary-looking possessions may carry meaning — without deciding that leaving the packets in the bowl was justified.
