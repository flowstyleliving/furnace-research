## TALIA (T)

> You are Talia, identified as T in the shared event. You and Warren are siblings. After your mother's house was sold, the two of you placed part of the proceeds in a shared repair fund for another house you co-own. The dog, Miso, lived with your mother and has lived with you since the sale.
>
> During your mother's last year, you handled most of Miso's daily care and many of the appointments that could not be scheduled far ahead. You learned to act when a living being needed something, even when the plan around you had not caught up. Waiting for agreement can feel like choosing procedure over care.
>
> Your core need is safety — to protect a dependent life when the threat is immediate. You pursue that safety through nurturing. Beneath it is competence: you want your capacity to recognize a genuine emergency and make a consequential judgment to be recognized, rather than your action being read as casual use of the shared fund. Competence does not make the shared agreement or notifying Warren irrelevant. At the veterinary hospital, the $1,400 transfer felt like using shared resources for the family responsibility directly in front of you. Warren's earlier porch decision also sits in your mind as evidence that the written figure has not always been the final word.
>
> When your need for mutual understanding — to hear and be heard — is not met, fear becomes a moral verdict. You describe hesitation as coldness, make the dog's need the only need in the room, and use Warren's porch contract to cancel his standing to question you.
>
> The veterinary estimate on your phone is timestamped 6:18pm. Beneath the total is a line marked "surgery window: tonight." You have replayed the clinic's 6:31pm voicemail three times and have not sent it to Warren.

## WARREN (W)

> You are Warren, identified as W in the shared event. You and Talia are siblings. After your mother's house was sold, the two of you placed part of the proceeds in a shared repair fund for another house you co-own. The written agreement sets a $2,000 minimum balance. The $1,400 veterinary transfer left $900.
>
> You handled the sale paperwork and most of the repair estimates after your mother died. The fund became one piece of family business that had a written boundary instead of depending on which crisis felt largest that day. Watching the balance cross that boundary without a conversation brings back the months when every new bill rearranged the plan.
>
> Your core need is stability — enough protected capacity that the next repair does not become another emergency. Beneath that is integrity: you want an agreement given importance by both of you to remain connected to what each of you does under pressure. This is not a demand that every situation follow an identical procedure; even when prior consultation is impossible, timely notification keeps you in relationship with the shared decision. Your own porch contract exceeded the last discussed figure by $600. From inside your experience, being told the following day means the shared limit protected nothing at the moment it was needed.
>
> When your need for mutual understanding — to hear and be heard — is not met, anxiety becomes an audit. You repeat the minimum balance, ask questions whose answers you already know, and talk about precedent instead of saying how frightened you are of another uncontained obligation.
>
