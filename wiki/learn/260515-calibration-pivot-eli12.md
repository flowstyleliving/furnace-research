## 🎯 The question

For most of v3.x we were trying to answer: **"Which single gauge — which exact combination of (metric, rank, generation step, sign) — detects rupture on every model?"** Three serious attempts in three weeks all failed. The pivot was realizing that question doesn't have an answer, and that *the absence of a universal answer is itself the finding*. This page explains the jump from "find the universal cell" to "calibrate the cell per ship and per route" and what we shipped to make that workable.

---

## 👓 The metaphor: the optometrist visit

Imagine you walk into an eye doctor's office and ask: *"Doctor, what's the right prescription for human eyes?"* The doctor would look at you funny. There isn't one. There's a right prescription for *you*. And not just for your eyes in general — there's one for *reading*, one for *driving*, maybe one for *computer work*. Same person, same eyes, different correction needed in different environments. 👁️

The optometrist's job isn't to discover the universal prescription. It's to **measure your eyes carefully** with a structured test, **pick the lens that lands sharpest** for the specific situation, **double-check by re-asking** ("better, 1 or 2?"), and **write a prescription that's good for this person, in this setting, for about 12 months**. They also write warnings on it: "patient has astigmatism — recheck if vision changes" or "marginal acuity at distance — retest in 6 months." A prescription with warnings is honest. A prescription without them might be a guess.

That's what `pri_calibrator.py` does, scaled to LLM rupture detection.

### Mapping the metaphor

| Eye exam | PRI calibration |
|---|---|
| 👁️ Your specific eyes | A specific model (Mistral-Nemo, Qwen 3 8B, …) |
| 📖 Reading vs 🚗 driving vs 💻 monitor | The exact deployment distribution (ANLI R1 vs R2 vs synthetic 2×2 vs TriviaQA paired) |
| 👓 The prescription itself | A `CalibrationProfile` JSON (winning `(cell, sign, threshold)`) |
| ➕➖ Diopter direction | `sign` field (locked from calibration data, not re-fit later) |
| 🔁 "Better, 1 or 2?" repeated checks | Out-of-bag bootstrap — re-runs cell selection inside each resample |
| ⚠️ "Recheck in 6 months" warnings | `winner_unstable`, `wide_ci`, `oob_low_auroc`, `insufficient_coverage` flags |
| 🪪 Prescription is for THESE frames | Pipeline-hash drift check: detector refuses to score if `pri_v2_mlx_pipeline.py` or the model snapshot changed |
| 🚫 Don't share glasses | Detector refuses if `output_projection_kind` (`lm_head` vs `tied_embed`) differs from calibration |

## 🧠 The conceptual shift

**Old framing (v1 → v3.2):** "There exists a privileged geometric channel — `pri_v2_lowrank32` or `null_ratio_post_rank1` or centered-Fisher — that detects contradiction commitment across model families. Our job is to find it."

**New framing (2026-05-12+):** "Rupture is real and *commitment-aligned*, but the geometric channel that picks it up varies by **(model, exact distribution)**. There is no single privileged cell. Our job is to ship a library that **measures your specific situation** and writes an honest prescription with deployability warnings."

The first framing produced three failed retrofits (centered-Fisher, adaptive-step, meta-classifier) because it kept asking the wrong question. The second framing produces a `CalibrationProfile` JSON that has a reproducible AUROC, a sign, a threshold, an honest CI, and warnings if any of those are shaky.

### Why "exact distribution" matters

The killer evidence came from the **33-profile ANLI sweep** on 2026-05-13: 11 cached models × ANLI R1/R2/R3 × n=50. ANLI rounds R1 / R2 / R3 are all "natural-language inference" — same broad task family. But R2 and R3 were generated by humans specifically adversarial to models that survived R1, then R3 specifically adversarial to models that survived R2. So they share *task family* but differ in *exact adversarial generation distribution*.

What happened: **the same model picked a different (cell, sign) on R1, R2, R3.** Example — Mistral-Nemo:
- R1 → Centered_post_rank2 @ step 1, sign `+`
- R2 → d_F_full @ step 1, sign `−`
- R3 → Centered_post_rank2 @ step 1, sign `+`

Same model. Same broad "NLI" task. Sign flipped between rounds, then flipped back. If you'd calibrated on R1 and deployed on R2, your detector would be **anti-correlated with truth** — worse than chance.

**Implication**: "task family" is not fine enough granularity. Calibration must match the *exact* deployment distribution.

### The "Fisher r=2 @ step 3" lesson

A separate piece of the same lesson. At n=5 (the original meta-classifier pilot), Phi-4 and Qwen 2.5 both picked `Fisher r=2 @ step 3` as their winning cell, with the same sign. That looked like the start of a universal "stable cell" hypothesis. At n=33 (the full ANLI sweep), Fisher r=2 @ step 3 has:
- Sign distribution: 17 positive, 15 negative (basically a coin flip)
- Median AUROC: 0.551 (chance is 0.5)
- Profiles where it won: 4 of 33

It was **noise that happened to align at small n**. Reporting it as a real cross-model finding would have been a false positive. Codex adversarial review caught this on the way: the 2026-05-12 in-flight `handcrafted_v4` rule had *two* methodological errors — `auroc_signed` let sign-fit on held-out labels, and the rule was authored after inspecting all 11 oracle cells (in-sample fit). Re-scoring with direction-preserving signs (fixed from training folds) dropped mean AUROC from 0.984 → 0.711 and min from 0.923 → 0.000.

## 📊 What we shipped

Two files, \~800 LOC together. Tests green, in repo.

### `pri_calibrator.py`
Takes a labeled `.jsonl` (one sample per line, with `label` field), runs the model on each sample via the existing `trace_sample` + `PRIComputer` pipeline, sweeps an 8-cell metric panel (`d_F_full`, `d_F_lowrank{r}`, `null_ratio_post_rank{r}`, `null_ratio_centered_post_rank{r}`, `kl_discharged`, at the relevant steps), and for each cell:
1. Picks the better sign (`+` AUROC vs `−` AUROC) **from training data only**.
2. Bootstraps 95% CI using **nested out-of-bag** resampling — re-runs cell selection inside each resample so the CI captures selection variance.
3. Reports `oob_auroc_median`, `winner_stability` (fraction of resamples that picked the same winning cell), `winner_counts` (full distribution).

Then writes a `CalibrationProfile` JSON with:
- Schema version (`v1.1`)
- `data_hash_sha256` (input data fingerprint)
- `pipeline_module_hash`, `calibrator_module_hash`, `plugins_module_hash`, `adapters_module_hash`, `model_snapshot_sha` (so a future deployment can verify nothing drifted)
- Winning cell + sign + threshold
- `calibration_stats`: in-sample AUROC, OOB AUROC + CI, winner stability
- `deployability_warnings`: list of flags (`winner_unstable` if stability < 0.7, `wide_ci` if CI width > 0.2, `oob_low_auroc` if OOB < 0.7, etc.)

### `pri_detector.py`
Loads a `CalibrationProfile`, exposes `Detector.from_profile()` / `.score(sample) → float` / `.predict(sample, threshold) → bool` / `.score_batch(samples)`. Safety rails fire on load:
- ❌ If `pipeline_module_hash` doesn't match current pipeline → refuse.
- ❌ If `output_projection_kind` (`lm_head` vs `tied_embed`) doesn't match → refuse.
- ❌ If schema version is older than current → refuse (forces re-calibration on schema upgrades).
- ⚠️ If profile has deployability warnings → log them at load time.
- ❌ If `predict()` called without an explicit threshold → refuse (no default; you must commit to one).

EOS-before-rupture is handled (some prompts terminate before the commit step is reached) — those samples get a sentinel score, not a silent zero.

**Self-test guarantee**: reported AUROC at calibration time and deployed AUROC on a fresh detector load are byte-exact equal (within 1e-3). This is enforced by `tests/test_pri_detector.py::TestRuntime::test_self_test_aurocs_match`. If they ever diverge, the test goes red.

## ⚠️ Caveats

- **n=50 is too small for most profiles.** The 33-profile ANLI sweep fired *at least one* deployability warning on 30 of 33 profiles at n=50. Only Mistral-Nemo R1 + Mistral-Nemo R3 + Qwen3-8B R1 came back clean. For real deployment, plan for n ≥ 200, or stronger priors on cell selection.
- **Sign-locking is from calibration data, not held-out.** It eliminates the *post-selection* sign-flip bug Codex caught, but it doesn't eliminate small-n sign noise — that's what `winner_stability` is for.
- **Detector won't catch model-update drift inside HuggingFace.** If `openai/gpt-x` gets re-uploaded under the same slug with new weights, the model snapshot SHA in strict mode will detect it. But that requires the SHA to have been recorded at calibration time *and* strict mode to be enabled at detector load.
- **"Per-(model, exact distribution)" is a constraint, not a feature.** A truly universal detector would be more valuable. The honest framing is that the *current* evidence doesn't support universality, not that we proved universality is impossible.

## 🎯 One-sentence takeaway

> The eye doctor doesn't dispense a universal prescription — they measure your eyes, pick the lens that lands sharpest for *your* setting, double-check under perturbation, and write warnings on the prescription if anything looks shaky. That's what `pri_calibrator.py` does for PRI on a specific model on a specific distribution; `pri_detector.py` is the prescription holder that refuses to dispense if the frames don't match. 👓
