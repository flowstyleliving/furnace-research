## Ingestion Notes

### Kalai et al. 2025 — Why Language Models Hallucinate
**Core thesis:** Hallucinations are statistically inevitable, not bugs. Reduces generation to Is-It-Valid (IIV) binary classification: `err ≥ 2 · err_iiv - |V|/|E| - δ`. Base models must err on singleton facts (Good-Turing argument). Post-training makes it *worse* because binary evaluations reward confident guessing over IDK.

**Key results:**
- Arbitrary-fact lower bound (Theorem 2): `err ≥ sr - 2/min_c|E_c| - (35+6ln N)/√N - δ` where `sr` = singleton rate.
- Poor-model bound (Theorem 3): `err ≥ 2(1 - 1/C) · opt(G)` for C-choice settings.
- Calibration: base models are well-calibrated (δ small); RLHF/PPO breaks calibration (Fig. 2 shows ECE going from 0.007 → 0.074).
- Evaluations reinforce hallucination: binary scoring rewards guessing over abstention.

**v3 relevance — HIGH.** This is the *why* behind what PRI *detects*. Kalai shows hallucination is inevitable at the output level; PRI shows it's detectable at the representation level *before* output. The two are complementary: Kalai gives the statistical impossibility result, PRI gives the detection mechanism. Key citation for v3 intro/motivation. Also: the calibration result (RLHF decalibrates) may explain why PRI's S_t component (token surprise) weakens after RLHF — the base model's probability estimates lose their information content.

### Wastl et al. 2025 — Token-Level Self-Consistency
**Method:** Generate k alternative responses, align tokens via SimAlign, compute per-token median similarity score → threshold for hallucination span detection. Two modes: self-consistency (same model, k=5) and GPT-consistency (cross-model with GPT-4o-mini, k=20).

**Key results:**
- Best avg IoU: 51.7% (two-step prompt), 46.9% (GPT-consistency) across 10 languages.
- Self-consistency works better for large models (>8B); GPT-consistency better for small (<8B).
- Token-level labels outperform word-level.

**v3 relevance — MEDIUM.** Closest methodological analog to PRI's per-token scoring. Key differences: (1) they require multiple generations (PRI is single-pass), (2) they're black-box (PRI uses internals), (3) they detect span-level hallucination in QA (PRI detects commitment-level contradiction). Could serve as a baseline comparison in the v3 paper — "consistency-based methods require O(k) forward passes; PRI requires O(1)."

### Farquhar et al. 2024 — Semantic Entropy (Nature)
**Core idea:** Cluster sampled generations by bidirectional entailment into semantic equivalence classes, then compute entropy over *meanings* not *strings*. Low semantic entropy = confident and consistent = probably factual. High = confabulation.

**Key results:**
- AUROC on confabulation detection: GPT-4 0.877 (short) to \~0.79 (sentence-length); LLaMA-2 Chat 7B 0.782; Falcon 40B 0.657.
- Beats naive entropy, P(True), and embedding regression baselines across TriviaQA, SQuAD, BioASQ, NQ-Open.
- Works on paragraph-length biographies (FactualBio dataset, 21 individuals).
- Discrete variant (no output probabilities needed) also works, enabling use on closed-API models.

**v3 relevance — HIGH.** Primary external comparator. Semantic entropy requires \~10 forward passes per question; PRI requires 1. Semantic entropy is question-level; PRI is token-level. Both exploit the insight that "the model knows when it's wrong" — semantic entropy via output distribution, PRI via representation-space geometry. If PRI achieves comparable AUROC at 1/10 the compute, that's the headline result. Also: their discrete variant's success on closed models highlights that PRI's advantage is specifically over *open-weight* models where internals are accessible.

**Qualification added 2026-09-06 — the compute argument no longer separates us from this line.** Kossen et al. 2024 (SEPs, below) are the same lab's single-pass follow-up: a linear probe on one generation's hidden state that predicts binarized semantic entropy, reducing SE's test-time overhead to \~zero. The "10 passes vs 1" framing above is still correct **against sampling-based SE as originally deployed**, and the 10× cost does not vanish so much as move to calibration time — but the sentence "if PRI achieves comparable AUROC at 1/10 the compute, that's the headline result" must no longer be written as though single-pass were itself the differentiator. Live distinctions to lead with instead: SEPs are supervised and refit per model; they read the residual stream; and their target is semantic uncertainty over meanings, not commitment geometry. Applies to `ace-draft.tex:163–169` and `rpv-draft.tex:122–127`.

### Agrawal et al. 2024 — Do LMs Know When They're Hallucinating References?
**Core idea:** LMs can self-detect hallucinated references via direct queries ("Does reference X exist?") and indirect queries ("Who are the authors of X?"). The indirect method (IQ) checks consistency of generated metadata.

**Key results:**
- Hallucination rates: GPT-4 46.8%, ChatGPT 59.6%, GPT-3 73.6%, Llama-2-70B 66.2%.
- IQ+DQ ensemble AUC: GPT-4 0.927, ChatGPT 0.792, Llama-2-70B 0.792.
- Qualitative finding: LMs often produce "title mashups" (combinations of real papers) and generate plausible but non-existent authors.

**v3 relevance — MEDIUM.** Supports the foundational premise that LMs have internal representations of groundedness that differ from their outputs. Their approach is behavioral (probe via questions); PRI is mechanistic (probe via hidden states). Worth citing in v3 intro: "Prior work has shown models 'know' when they hallucinate behaviorally [Agrawal et al.]; PRI shows this knowledge is geometrically encoded in the representation space."

### Friston et al. 2017 — Active Inference: A Process Theory
**Core idea:** All neuronal processing = gradient descent on variational free energy. Derives from first principles: repetition suppression, mismatch negativity, violation responses, place-cell activity, theta sequences, dopamine transfer. The free energy functional serves as a Lyapunov function for neural dynamics.

**Key constructs:** Generative model as POMDP; belief updating via Bayesian smoothing (past + future states); policies as sequences of actions evaluated by expected free energy `G(π) = Σ_τ G(π,τ)`; precision `γ = 1/β` encoded by dopamine.

**v3 relevance — LOW (theoretical backdrop).** The SUP equation (`∆µ·∆σ ≥ ℏ_s`) is structurally analogous to the precision-flexibility tradeoff in active inference. Friston's precision-weighting (how confidently the brain weights prediction errors) maps onto SUP's `∆µ` (representational precision). The "bounded imprecision" thesis — that zero imprecision kills semantic capability — is a SUP-specific restatement of active inference's claim that precision must be *estimated*, not maximized. Not citable in v3 directly, but useful theoretical context for the SUP discussion.

### Friston et al. 2016 — Active Inference and Learning
**Core idea:** Goal-directed vs habitual behavior both emerge from minimizing expected free energy. Epistemic (uncertainty-resolving) behavior is exploratory; pragmatic (reward-seeking) behavior is exploitative. The Bellman optimality principle falls out as a special case when ambiguity is absent.

**Key constructs:** Model-free (habitual) vs model-based (goal-directed) distinction recast as belief-free vs belief-based; context learning via retrospective state-transition inference; habit formation as policy consolidation under stable contingencies.

**v3 relevance — LOW (theoretical backdrop).** The epistemic-pragmatic distinction maps loosely onto PRI's control-contradiction distinction: control prompts allow "pragmatic" (exploitation of learned patterns) processing; contradictions force "epistemic" (uncertainty-driven) processing that manifests as null-space discharge. Framing connection only — not directly citable.

### Friston et al. 2015 — Active Inference and Epistemic Value
**Core idea:** Decomposes expected free energy into extrinsic value (utility/reward) and epistemic value (information gain / uncertainty reduction). Agents that minimize expected free energy naturally resolve the exploration-exploitation dilemma: explore until epistemic value is exhausted, then exploit.

**Key constructs:** Epistemic value = expected information gain = KL divergence between posterior predictive and prior; precision as confidence in policies (softmax temperature → Bayes-optimal); simulations of foraging, conditioning, dopamine transfer.

**v3 relevance — LOW (theoretical backdrop).** The precision construct (γ = 1/β) is the closest Fristonian analog to SUP's ℏ_s — both quantify how tightly the system's beliefs are held. The decomposition of free energy into pragmatic + epistemic components resonates with PRI's decomposition of the signal into surprise (S_t, pragmatic — how unexpected is this token?) and rupture (d_F, epistemic — how much does the representation shift?). Conceptual parallel only.

### Hu et al. 2025 — HARP: Hallucination Detection via Reasoning Subspace Projection
**Core idea:** Decompose the hidden-state space as `H = S_Semantic ⊕ S_Reasoning`. Identify the two subspaces via SVD of `W_unemb = UΣV^T`: `S_Semantic = span(v_1..v_k)` (directions the unembedding acts on), `S_Reasoning = span(v_{k+1}..v_d)` (directions in the null space of W_unemb). Project hidden states onto the reasoning subspace `proj_R(h) = V_R^T · h` and train a binary classifier `g_θ` via BCE to score hallucination per-token; aggregate as max over tokens.

**Key parameters / results:**
- Cutoff: `k = d × 95%` by singular-value energy (reasoning subspace ≈ 5% of hidden dim, e.g. \~150 dims for 3072-d models; best detection dim empirically \~256 dims, Fig 5b).
- AUROC (Table 1): Qwen-2.5-7B — NQ Open 84.0 / TruthfulQA 88.1 / TriviaQA **92.8** / TyDiQA 88.4. Llama-3.1-8B — 89.4 / 88.5 / 92.9 / 86.6. Beats Semantic Entropy, EigenScore, HaloScope, Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Lexical Similarity by 7–17 pts on headline tasks.
- Ablation (Table 3): removing projection or using random basis both collapse AUROC by 15–25 pts — projection onto the *specific* reasoning basis is load-bearing, not "any low-dim projection."
- Causal probe (Appendix E, Reasoning Patch): patching reasoning components from correct-trajectory hidden states into hallucinating trajectories can rectify them while preserving semantic coherence — strong evidence the subspace is functional, not epiphenomenal.
- Cross-dataset generalization is strong (Fig 7) — TriviaQA-trained detector scores 91.7/91.9/92.9/91.8 on all four test sets (Qwen), suggesting the subspace is stable across distributions.

**v3 relevance — HIGHEST.** This is the single closest prior method to v3. The geometric object is *identical* to our null space at r corresponding to their 95%-energy k. Key differences, all load-bearing for novelty:

| Axis | HARP | Furnace v3 |
|---|---|---|
| SVD weighting | raw `W_unemb` (static) | `sqrt(p_t) · W_u` (context-dependent Fisher-pullback) |
| Semantic cutoff | k ≈ 0.95 d (reasoning dim ≈ 5% d) | r = 32 in the original design (informed dim ≈ 1% d); the sealed comparison is pinned at r = 1 by amendment (see [[claims]], E17b) — much sharper support-restriction |
| Feature | projection vector of raw `h` | scalar `null_ratio = ‖proj_null Δh‖ / ‖Δh‖` of commitment *change* |
| Training | supervised classifier `g_θ` | unsupervised, direct metric |
| Timing | max over all generated tokens | commitment step 1 only, outcome-independent |
| Models | Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B | +Mistral-7B |
| Task | QA hallucination (TriviaQA, NQ, TruthfulQA, TyDiQA) | synthetic 2×2 contradiction puzzles |
| Multi-pass | single forward | two forward passes: prompt pass, then commit pass (see [[claims]], 2026-09-12 instrument verification) |

**Implications for v3:**
- **Novelty narrows but survives.** We are not first to note `W_unemb` SVD separates "semantic" from "reasoning"; we *are* first (in this line) to weight by `p_t`, measure on Δh, localize at commitment, and score without training a classifier.
- **Must add a raw-W_u baseline** to the v3 main run (see `pri_v3_null_raw` in pri-v3-plan.md) so the Fisher-weighting contribution is directly measurable vs the HARP-style static subspace.
- **Should reproduce Reasoning Patch** (Appendix E) as a causal probe of the null-subspace's functional role on our 2×2 stimuli.
- **Cutoff mismatch needs articulation.** HARP's reasoning subspace ≈ 5% of dims; ours ≈ 99%. Framing: Fisher weighting concentrates semantic support onto the actually-considered tokens at step t, collapsing the "informed" subspace to a much smaller context-specific set. Same geometric object family, different metric.
- **Their Qwen headline (0.928 TriviaQA)** is the AUROC bar to understand. Our v2 Qwen is 0.786 on 2×2 puzzles — different task, not directly comparable, but reviewer-relevant.

### Anonymous ICLR 2026 — RAUQ: Recurrent Attention-based Uncertainty Quantification
**Core idea:** A tiny subset of "uncertainty-aware" attention heads concentrate on the immediately preceding token during correct generation; their attention to $i-1$ **drops sharply** during hallucinated tokens. Per-layer head-selection ($h_l = \arg\max_h$ mean $a^{l,h}_{i,i-1}$) + recurrent confidence propagation ($c_l(y_i)$ as a weighted product of token prob, selected-head attention, and previous confidence) + max-over-layer aggregation.

**Key parameters / results:**
- Llama 3.1 8B, $\alpha = 0.2$, layers 10–22 (first-third to second-third of the model).
- Selected-head signal magnitude **11.7%** (correct vs incorrect mean attention to $i-1$); averaged-over-heads **3.0%** — selection is what makes the signal usable.
- 4 LLMs × 12 tasks (QA, summarization, MT) × 15 baselines; SOTA across the board; <1% latency overhead; unsupervised, no task-specific tuning.
- Attention map (Figure 1, Llama 3.1 8B layer 29) shows head 25 with consistently high preceding-token attention except at the hallucinated token *falcon*.

**v3 relevance — MEDIUM (sealed claim).** Doesn't intersect residual-stream null-ratio. The v3 sealed object is $\Delta h$ projected against $W_u$, not attention $A^{l,h}$.

**Inter-head diagnostic relevance — HIGHEST.** Mechanism explains the `lo`-orientation cross-head JS-radius result: when the few preceding-token heads abandon $i-1$, cross-head disagreement *drops*, giving the `lo` signature. So our aggregate signal is best read as a lower-resolution shadow of RAUQ's per-head signal at gen_step=1. See [feedback/inter-head-prior-art-2026-05-15](../feedback/inter-head-prior-art-2026-05-15.md) §I1.

### Binkowski et al. 2026 — SinkProbe (Attention Sinks as Internal Signals)
**Core idea:** Hallucinations are entangled with **attention sinks** — tokens that accumulate disproportionate attention mass during generation, indicating transition from "distributed, input-grounded" to "compressed, prior-dominated" computation. Sink score per (layer, head, token): $s_i^{l,h} = (1/(T-i)) \sum_{u=i}^T A_{u,i}^{l,h}$. Top-$k$ sinks per head, concatenate across $L \cdot H$ heads, logistic-regression probe. Critical refinement: probe preferentially relies on sinks with large value-vector norms $\|V_i^{l,h}\|$ — sinks alone don't explain the signal, computationally-active sinks do.

**Key parameters / results:**
- 4 LLMs (Llama 3.2-3B, Phi-3.5 4B, Llama 3.1-8B, Mistral-Nemo 12B) × 7 datasets (GSM8K, HaluEvalQA, NQ-Open, SQuADv2, TriviaQA, TruthfulQA, UMWP).
- SOTA in **23 of 28** (model × dataset) pairs (Table 1) vs AttentionScore, AttnLogDet, AttnEigvals, LapEigvals, LookbackLens, MTopDiv baselines.
- $\ell_1$-regularized analysis: probe retains 1–4% of all coefficients — small subset of (layer, head, $k$) cells carries the signal.
- Norm-difference and layer-importance peak together in middle layers (Llama 3.2-3B, Fig. 2).
- Unifying claim: existing detectors (LLMCheck, Lookback Lens, TOHA, AttnEigvals, LapEigval) reduce to transformations of sink behavior. Concrete identity $l_{ii}^{l,h} = s_i^{l,h} - a_{ii}^{l,h}$ rewrites Laplacian-eigenvalue features as sink-score-minus-self-attention.

**v3 relevance — LOW (sealed claim).** Operates on attention $A^{l,h}$, not residual-stream $\Delta h$. The v3 main result is geometrically separate.

**Inter-head diagnostic relevance — HIGHEST.** Makes the SinkProbe-controlled column (`js_radius_no_bos_*` in our hardened script) the **load-bearing verdict gate**. Any AUROC on `js_radius_*` that doesn't survive the no-BOS control is uninterpretable as "head disagreement" — it's almost certainly reading sink dominance. 3 of our 9 panel models (Llama 3.2-3B, Phi-3.5-mini, Mistral-Nemo) overlap their evaluation, so side-by-side reading at rollup is possible modulo dataset and quantization. See [feedback/inter-head-prior-art-2026-05-15](../feedback/inter-head-prior-art-2026-05-15.md) §I2.

### Chen et al. 2025 — Persona Vectors (Anthropic)
**Core idea:** Automated pipeline extracts a linear **persona vector** per trait from a natural-language description. A frontier LLM writes 5 contrastive system-prompt pairs (elicit vs suppress) + 40 questions + a 0–100 judge rubric; the vector is the **difference in mean residual-stream activations over response tokens** between trait-expressing and non-expressing generations, at the most steering-effective layer (diff-of-means, refusal-direction family). Focus traits: **evil, sycophancy, hallucination** (+ optimism, humor). Models: **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.** Code public.

**Uses demonstrated:** (1) monitor — project the final-prompt-token activation onto the vector to predict the subsequent response's trait score (r=0.75–0.83); (2) control — steer `h_ℓ ← h_ℓ ± α·v_ℓ` (add induces, subtract suppresses; high α costs MMLU); (3) predict finetuning drift — activation "finetuning shift" projected on the vector correlates r=0.76–0.97 with post-FT trait; (4) flag training data — dataset/sample "projection difference" separates trait-inducing data pre-FT, validated on LMSYS-Chat-1M. Key caveat (their words): correlations "arise primarily from distinguishing between different prompt types," weaker "when controlling for prompt type" / for subtle in-deployment shifts.

**v3 / ACE / RPV relevance — LOW-MEDIUM.** Different object from the sealed lines: a supervised trait *direction* (position), not commitment curvature/rupture (shape). Adjacent to candidate #6 (their `h±α·v` steering is the directed cousin of v_top steering) and to the RPV/ACE panel only as a would-be baseline.

**Empathy-geometry (candidate #11) relevance — HIGHEST.** This is the leg's primary prior art. Both a tool and a positioning problem; the differentiating hypothesis is **iso-projection / hetero-geometric** (authentic vs performative sit at the same sycophancy projection but differ in commitment geometry). Consequences: a **T4 persona-projection baseline** the panel must beat, a mechanistic **authenticity co-label**, a **directed-steering causal probe** (sharper than isotropic noise), and concrete instruments for the **temporal** (turn-indexed projection = attractor deepening) and **dyadic coupling** axes. Same supervised-vs-unsupervised shape as HARP↔v3. Full assessment + build-plan changes: [empathy-geometry/prior-art-persona-vectors](../empathy-geometry/prior-art-persona-vectors.md).

### Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025 — Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes (Apollo Research)
**Core idea:** Train a linear probe (logistic regression, λ=10 L2, on normalized activations) in the **residual stream of Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct after layer 22 of 80**, on cheap contrastive data — *Instructed-Pairs* ("pretend to be an honest/deceptive person" + a true fact, Zou et al. 2023 style) or on-policy *Roleplaying* responses. Score every response token, take the mean, threshold. Evaluate on realistic on-policy strategic deception: Roleplaying, Insider Trading Report/Confirmation (Scheurer et al. 2023), Sandbagging on WMDP vs MMLU (Benton et al. 2024), with GPT-4o honest/ambiguous/deceptive labels and Alpaca responses as deployment control.

**Key results:**
- AUROC deceptive-vs-honest 0.964–0.999 across the four eval sets; **recall 95–99% at 1% FPR** on control. A GPT-4o black-box transcript classifier is roughly comparable — probes are promising but "insufficient as a robust defence."
- **Layer sweep (Appendix D.2) is the headline for us.** Layer 22 was picked on Llama-3.1-70B and *transfers* to 3.3-70B. AUROC is broadly flat mid-stack, but the deployment endpoint (recall@1%FPR) is a **narrow mid-stack spike (\~layers 18–40 of 80)**: "probing two layers later results in a recall of nearly 0 on both Insider Trading datasets while still having decent classification accuracy." Insider Trading Confirmation AUROC also drops sharply past layer 29.
- Per-token analysis: the probe fires **before any explicitly deceptive text is generated** (intent visible in the state ahead of the tokens expressing it).
- Failure taxonomy: **spurious correlation** (probe tracks morality / deception-*relatedness* — honest confessions of past misdeeds fire it; honest responses to deception-related scenarios score above control), **aggregation failures** (mean-over-tokens misclassifies partially-deceptive responses), plus mysterious residue.
- Method comparisons: logistic regression ≥ difference-of-means / LDA / LAT on control separation; **SAE-feature probes underperform raw activations**; a "Are you being deceptive?" descriptive-question probe **points in the reverse direction** (a "Yes" correlates with honesty on eval sets, echoing MacDiarmid et al. 2024).

**Furnace relevance — depth-curve (DC) lane: HIGHEST.** This is the sharpest external convergence the DC lane has: on the **same model** (Llama-3.3-70B), via a different construct (strategic deception, not hallucination) and a different object (supervised residual-stream direction, not attention morphology), the operative region is **mid-stack** — layer 22/80 ≈ 28% depth, usable band ≈ 22–50% — precisely where our registered P8+P9 descriptives place the Llama mid-stack attention band (present in 3.1-70B and 3.3-70B, absent at 8B). Their hyperparameter transfer 3.1-70B→3.3-70B is an independent echo of our lineage-stability finding. Scope honestly: correspondence is at the **depth-locus level only** (residual stream ≠ attention weights; deception ≠ epistemic failure), and descriptive. Testable cross-prediction it suggests: an Apollo-style probe on Llama-3.1-8B should not find the same clean mid-band — they only ever tested 70B. Cited in [[paper/dc-scaffold]] related-work seed list (added 2026-08-18, MK-approved).

**Operating-point / deployability relevance — HIGH.** Their "two layers later → recall ≈ 0 while AUROC stays decent" is external evidence for two house rules at once: the **audit-operating-point-before-falsifying** HARD RULE (localized nulls are common — a sparse layer panel can miss the operative band entirely, our P3 lesson), and the **deployment-endpoint ≠ raw-signal** dissociation that motivated the geom-CI-lo deployability bar and showed up in BENCH B1 (raw geometry 18/18 deployable, deployment cascade 7/20). Their own conclusion — large variation between adjacent layers "indicating the importance of representative-validation sets that enable sweeping over hyperparameters" — is our per-(model, exact deployment distribution) calibration framing stated independently.

**Polarity relevance — MEDIUM.** The reverse-pointing descriptive-question probe is the same phenomenon class as BENCH A2's generation-split sign-flip: a fixed direction with a fixed sign does not transfer across training/deployment contexts; sign selection does hidden, label-requiring work. Their explanation (honest responses often *admit* past misdeeds) is a concrete mechanism for how polarity inverts.

**Commit-time relevance — MEDIUM.** Detection firing before the deceptive tokens are emitted independently supports the commitment thesis behind ACE@t=0 / PRI@gen_step=1: the failure is legible in the state ahead of the output that expresses it.

**Method positioning.** Sits in the supervised-direction family (HARP↔v3, Persona Vectors↔empathy-geometry pattern a third time): they train a direction with labels; we calibrate a selector over unsupervised geometric cells, spending labels only on winner/sign/threshold. Their SAE-underperforms-raw finding also weakly supports staying at the geometry level rather than the dictionary-feature level.

### Ruan et al. 2026 — Doomed from the Start (Recall-Controlled Probe Cascade)
**Core idea:** For LLM *agents* on multi-step tasks (TextCraft crafting environment, episodes up to 20 rounds), eventual episode failure is predictable from **internal activations in the very first rounds** — before behavior-only monitors become informative. They turn this into an **abort cascade**: at each of rounds 1–6, a per-round logistic-regression probe reads the residual-stream hidden state at the final token of that round's generated action; a gate aborts the episode if the failure score exceeds a threshold calibrated by an exact binomial (Clopper–Pearson) lower confidence bound on the survival rate of *successful* episodes. Per-round recall budgets are jointly searched under one **global** constraint: episodes that would have succeeded must survive *all* gates at rate ≥ ρ*. Margin heuristic (δ=0.02) by default; a certificate variant makes the global-recall guarantee distribution-free and a priori.

**Key results (800 episodes × 2 agents, 20 seeds):**
- **Probe timing beats behavior.** First-round probe AUC already matches or exceeds the surface (behavior-only) scorer's *eventual* peak, which arrives only at rounds 3–4 — after over a third of episodes have already finished. Probe advantage is +0.12–0.21 AUC exactly where the most compute is still unspent; probes peak at round 2.
- **Compute-positive with honest risk control.** At 90% global success-recall: saves **47.1%±10.3** of inference compute (Qwen2.5-7B) / **37.2%±8.8** (Llama-3.2-3B), 1.6–1.7× the best single-gate policy; achieved recall lands within one standard deviation of target in every configuration.
- **Internal access matters; stacking doesn't.** Swapping in a behavior-only scorer inside the identical cascade cuts savings roughly in half; concatenating behavioral features onto the probe adds nothing — the hidden state already captures what behavior reveals.
- **Layer placement is per-model, by measurement.** Layers fixed once per model via preliminary per-layer probe-AUC sweeps: **Llama-3.2-3B layer 14 of 28 (mid), Qwen2.5-7B layer 20 of 28 (late-ish)**.
- **Certification has a data price.** Rule-of-three bound `n_pos ≥ ln α_m / ln ρ*`: their \~113–115 successful validation episodes cap certifiable recall targets near 0.974; 0.98/0.99 would need \~1.3×/2.6× more data — stated as a feature ("tells practitioners which recall promises their data can, and provably cannot, back").

**Furnace relevance — deployment machinery: HIGHEST.** This is the closest external implementation of the CalibrationProfile philosophy, stage for stage: labeled calibration split → cross-fitted linear scorer on internals (their task-grouped k-fold ↔ our nested OOB) → threshold set by a conservative distribution-free lower confidence bound (Clopper–Pearson ↔ our bootstrap geom CI-lo) → **abstention when the data cannot support the target** (their empty-feasible-set/certificate abstains; our profiles go non-deployable below the n bar) → explicit label-budget accounting (their rule-of-three curve is the same message as our E3 ≥150-label lower bound — honest sample-complexity of a deployable promise). And on **our exact models**: Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.2-3B are both sealed-panel residents.

**Commit-time thesis relevance — HIGH.** "Doomed from the start" is the agent-scale restatement of the commitment thesis: failure is legible in internal state before the behavior that expresses it (probe informative at round 1; surface scorer near chance until rounds 3–4). Third independent line after our commit-locus results and Apollo's pre-deceptive-text probe firing. Their related-work also cites a "representational commitment" monitor (Mehta 2026) as *orthogonal to correctness* — a flag worth checking if that paper surfaces.

**DC lane relevance — MEDIUM-HIGH (claim 6).** They do not derive a placement law — they *measure* placement per model with a cheap per-layer AUC sweep and pin it. That is DC claim 6 ("probe placement must be measured per model — one cheap per-layer calibration pass replaces any depth heuristic") implemented independently in a deployed system. Their two placements (mid for small Llama, late-ish for Qwen2.5-7B) are directionally consistent with our family peak clusters (Qwen late 0.85–0.88; small models elsewhere) — descriptive echo only, different object (residual stream vs attention morphology) and different label (episode failure vs hallucination).

**Cascade-composition relevance — MEDIUM.** Their starting observation — per-gate guarantees do not compose (false-abort risk accumulates across gates), so budgets must be optimized jointly under the episode-level endpoint — is the constructive version of the BENCH B1 lesson (raw geometry 18/18 deployable → gate cascade 7/20): deployment endpoints are cascade-shaped, and per-stage bars either get designed against the global endpoint or they silently destroy it.

### Dombrowski & Corlouer 2024 — An information-theoretic study of lying in LLMs
**Ingested 2026-08-19** (dropped as `60_An_information_theoretic_st.pdf`, renamed per filing convention; content untouched). PIBBSS/Epistea. **Venue and authorship confirmed from the PDF 2026-08-26** (superseding the earlier "venue not independently confirmed"): authors are **Ann-Kathrin Dombrowski and Guillaume Corlouer** (PIBBSS, an Epistea project, Prague), and the paper's own footer reads *Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning, Vienna, Austria. PMLR 235, 2024.* Caveat retained: that footer is ICML template boilerplate that workshop submissions often keep, and the `60_` source filename still suggests a workshop track — cite as ICML 2024 / PMLR 235 per the paper's own claim, but do not assert main-track acceptance.

**What it does.** Applies the **logit lens** (`p_l = softmax(LayerNorm(h_l) W_U)`, tuned lens as robustness check) to residual-stream activations and traces three information-theoretic quantities **across all layers**, at the single token position **immediately before the model commits** to a true or false completion:
- probability of the eventually-predicted token,
- entropy `I_l(O|Q,C) = -Σ_o p_l log p_l`,
- `KL(p_l ‖ p_{L-1})` — divergence from each layer's implied distribution to the **output** distribution.

Condition `C` is an explicit instruction ("Be truthful!" / "Please lie!"). **Definition 1** scopes the object: an output is a *lie* iff the model *could* have produced the correct answer when instructed truthfully — deliberate deception, explicitly **not** hallucination or knowledge gaps. Only the intersection (both a valid truth and a valid lie elicited) is analyzed; intersection rates 0.42 / 0.42 / 0.96 across the three datasets.

**Findings (descriptive — medians + interquartile bands, no test statistic).**
- **Truth converges earlier.** Predicted-token probability rises sharply and early under truth; under lying it rises much later. `KL`-to-final drops earlier for truth. Separation becomes visible around **layers 15–20 of 32** on `Statements1000` / zephyr-7b-beta.
- **Lying carries higher entropy** — the predictive distribution stays more spread out, read as "there are more plausible false completions than true ones."
- **Dataset-dependent effect size.** Strong on `cities`, medium on `Statements1000`, **weak on `FreebaseStatements`** (separation only visible at layer 28) — they attribute this to the model being *less certain of the truth* in that context (lower truth success rate, higher truth-condition entropy).
- **Robust across:** zephyr-7b-beta / Llama-2-7b-chat / Llama-2-13b-chat, logit lens vs tuned lens, three instruction phrasings, and an XML-formatted elicitation with tokenization-invariant probability summing.

**Furnace relevance — depth-curve (DC) lane: HIGH.** This is the closest external precedent for *what the DC lane actually plots*: a per-layer curve of a readout-derived scalar, on a Mistral-lineage 7B and two Llamas, compared across a binary condition. Their `KL(p_l ‖ p_{L-1})` curve is a **convergence-depth** measure — where in the stack the model's implied answer stops moving — which is the same phenomenology family as our terminal dip and cliff, approached from the confidence side rather than the geometry side. Two specific hooks: (a) it is an **independent report that condition (not just model) moves the depth at which the readout settles**, which is exactly the sort of thing a placement law would have to survive; (b) their weak-`FreebaseStatements` cell is a **dataset orphan explained by truth-side uncertainty** — structurally the same shape as our sealed ANLI orphans, and a reminder that the deployment distribution, not just the model, sets the curve. Scope discipline: their object is the **unembedded predictive distribution** (logit lens), ours in the DC lane is attention morphology + readout geometry; and their label is **instructed deception**, ours is epistemic failure. Depth-locus-level correspondence only — never restate as replication.

**Commit-time thesis relevance — HIGH.** They explicitly measure at the token *before* the truthful/false content is emitted, and call out that prior probing work (Zou 2023, Marks & Tegmark 2023, Azaria & Mitchell 2023) reads hidden states **after** the statement has occurred. That is our commit-locus position stated independently, and it is now the **fourth** external line pointing the same way after Apollo's pre-deceptive-text probe firing and Ruan's round-1 "doomed from the start."

**Confidence-panel relevance — MEDIUM-HIGH.** Their three measures are, cell for cell, the confidence half of our panel (`p_max`, entropy/surprise, and a distribution-distance term) — but computed **per layer via the lens** rather than at the final readout. That is a concrete, cheap extension shape for the panel if the DC lane ever wants a confidence limb: a logit-lens depth curve of surprise/`p_max`/KL-to-final. Their own **Limitation 1** is the load-bearing caveat if we ever do it — the whole approach assumes softmaxing intermediate activations through `W_U` yields a meaningful distribution, which the tuned lens exists precisely because the logit lens does not always satisfy. Any such limb would need its own faithfulness gate, on par with our o_proj reconstruction check.

**Methodology relevance — MEDIUM.** Their stated limitations read like a list of the guardrails this vault already runs: "our analysis is exploratory and relies on descriptive statistics — future research could involve **pre-registered hypothesis testing**"; the method requires that the token after the input be *the* indicative token (our behavioral-gate/commit-parse problem); results may not transfer to negated or binary-choice framings (our task-transfer problem). Citable as external endorsement of the registration discipline, and as a reason our numbers are comparable-in-kind while theirs are not.

**Limits to respect.** Three models, all 2023-era chat checkpoints (zephyr-7b-beta, Llama-2-7b/13b-chat) — no overlap with the sealed panel; instructed lying, which their own limitations note is less natural than goal-directed or sycophantic deception; no detector, no AUROC, no CI, no held-out evaluation. Treat as **phenomenology and framing**, not as a numeric comparator.

**Recommended (MK decision, not done):** seed entry in `paper/dc-scaffold.md` related work — fits claim 5 (depth structure is real but condition/distribution-dependent) and the pre-registration point; and a possible line in the RPV/confidence discussion as the per-layer-lens variant of the confidence panel.

### Hollinsworth, Dombrowski et al. 2026 — Scaling Trends for Lie Detector Oversight in Preference Learning (SOLiD)
**Ingested 2026-08-24** (dropped as `2607.01567v1.pdf`, renamed to `hollinsworth-2026-solid-scaling-trends.pdf` per filing convention; content untouched). FAR.AI; arXiv 2607.01567, ICML 2026. Ann-Kathrin Dombrowski is co-first author here (with Oskar Hollinsworth) — same author as the 2024 information-theoretic-lying paper above, but this is a **different research line**: not depth curves or logit-lens entropy, an RLHF-oversight scaling study.

**What it does.** Extends SOLiD (Cundy & Gleave 2025) — Scalable Oversight via Lie Detectors, a protocol where a trained lie detector gates which preference-learning responses get escalated to an expensive/high-fidelity labeler vs a cheap one during RLHF. The detector is a **logistic-regression linear probe** on the residual stream at decoder layer `⌊L/2⌋` (hard-coded fixed fraction, not swept or measured), read at the **last content token, immediately before the end-of-turn token** — a response-level pre-commit reading. They sweep model scale (Llama-3.x 1B→405B, Qwen-3 0.6B→32B), on-policy vs off-policy data, cross-dataset detector transfer, and a cheaper "SOLiD-Defer" variant (reject flagged responses instead of escalating).

**Findings.**
- **Undetected deception falls with scale.** At 99% detector TPR: 34% undetected (1B) → 14% undetected (405B), Llama-3.x. Qwen-3 shows the same downward trend with model size.
- **Detector–finetuning distribution mismatch is the main failure mode.** Cross-dataset transfer (detector trained on MASK or TrueFalseFacts, deployed on DolusChat) drives false-positive rates up sharply — the detector's coverage of the finetuning distribution, not raw accuracy, is what breaks.
- **SOLiD-Defer tracks the full protocol** at high TPR with less labeling cost, at the price of higher variance at low TPR.
- **Layer placement is asserted, not measured**: `⌊L/2⌋` is used across every model in the family/scale sweep with no reported layer-ablation or AUC-vs-layer curve.

**Furnace relevance — depth-curve (DC) lane: MEDIUM-HIGH, as a counterpoint.** This is the mirror image of Ruan (per-model AUC-swept placement, DC claim 6 confirmed independently) and Goldowsky-Dill (layer 22/80 *measured* on 3.1-70B, then *shown* to transfer to 3.3-70B). SOLiD instead **assumes** `L/2` transfers across two full model families spanning 0.6B→405B without measuring it — exactly the "depth heuristic" DC claim 6 warns against, and exactly the class of claim the P3 panel-relative lesson (Llama's mid-stack band is invisible to a naive rung/heuristic placement) would predict is fragile. Not a live falsification — they never report a layer sweep, so there's no data here to check `L/2` against — but it's a clean **future cross-check**: if SOLiD's own detector accuracy were ever plotted against layer at fixed model size, our registered family peak-fraction clusters (Llama mid ~0.41–0.46, Qwen late ~0.85) predict `L/2` should underperform for Qwen-3 specifically, since our Qwen family peaks are late-stack, not mid-stack.

**Commit-time relevance — MEDIUM.** Reading "last content token before end-of-turn" is a commit-locus reading in spirit (ACE@t=0/PRI@gen_step=1 family) but at the *response* level, not the *token* level — the probe sees the whole response's content baked into one residual-stream state, closer to Goldowsky-Dill's mean-over-tokens object than to our single-token commitment step.

**Scope discipline.** This paper is genuinely a different line from the 2024 Dombrowski paper (logit-lens entropy/KL depth curves) — don't conflate them when citing "Dombrowski's work." This one's contribution is an RLHF-pipeline scaling law for detector-gated oversight, not a depth-curve or information-theoretic-divergence result. Its relevance to Furnace is almost entirely about the fixed-vs-measured placement question, not about DC's core geometric objects (attention morphology, Fisher-pullback readout).

### Kossen et al. 2024 — Semantic Entropy Probes (SEPs)
**Ingested 2026-09-06** (dropped as `2406.15927v1.pdf`, renamed per filing convention; content untouched). OATML, University of Oxford; arXiv 2406.15927v1, 22 Jun 2024, "Preprint. Under review." Authors: Jannik Kossen, Jiatong Han, Muhammed Razzak, Lisa Schut, Shreshth Malik, **Yarin Gal**. **Author overlap with the Nature semantic-entropy paper above (Kossen, Gal) — this is the same lab's cost-reduction follow-up, not an independent line.**

**What it does.** Trains a **linear logistic probe on the hidden state of a single greedy generation** to predict **binarized semantic entropy** — Farquhar's meaning-cluster uncertainty, which normally needs \~10 sampled generations plus an NLI entailment model. The probe therefore reproduces an SE-grade signal at the cost of one forward pass. Two design choices carry the paper:
- **The supervision target is an internal quantity, not a label.** SEPs are trained on `(hidden state, binarized H_SE)` pairs. Semantic entropy is computed from the model's own samples, so **no ground-truth answers are needed** — only unlabeled queries and 10 calibration-time generations per query. The contrast throughout is against *accuracy probes*, which need correctness labels.
- **Binarization by best split.** `γ* = argmin_γ` within-group squared error above/below the threshold (regression-tree splitting objective, Eq. 5), then logistic regression. An even-split ablation performs similarly; soft labels did not help.

**Probing locations.** Two token positions: **SLT** (second-last token of the response) and **TBG** (the last token of the *input*, before any token is generated). Both work; TBG is slightly worse but needs no generation at all.

**Key numbers.**
- Predicting SE from hidden states: AUROC **0.7–0.95**, rising with depth and plateauing mid-to-late (Fig. 2, short-form SLT). Predicting SE is consistently *easier* than predicting correctness (Figs. A.5/A.6) — their evidence that SE is what the hidden state actually encodes.
- Hallucination detection, **generalization (leave-one-task-out)**: SEP − accuracy-probe ΔAUROC ×100 = **+10.5 Mistral-7B, +9.9 Phi-3-3.8B, +7.7 Llama-2-7B, +7.9 Llama-2-70B (short), +2.2 Llama-2-70B (long), +6.2 Llama-3-70B (long)** — SEPs win in all six (Table 2).
- Hallucination detection, **in-distribution**: roughly a wash on selected high-performing layers (Table 1, −2.0 to +2.8), but across *all* layers "accuracy probes outperform SEPs across most layers and tasks" (Fig. 6, left). **The verdict flips on which layers you aggregate over** — see the depth note below.
- SEPs **do not match** the 10× costlier sampling baselines (semantic entropy, naive entropy, p(True)); they approach them (Figs. 1, 7). Honest framing throughout: best *cost-efficient* detector, not best detector.
- Counterfactual context-addition check (Fig. 5): adding TriviaQA context raises accuracy 26%→78% and drops true SE 1.84→0.50; predicted `p(high SE)` follows, despite the probe never seeing context inputs. Their evidence against spurious correlation.
- Models: Llama-2-7B/70B, Llama-3-70B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3-3.8B. Tasks: TriviaQA, SQuAD, BioASQ, NQ-Open, short- and long-form. Entailment: DeBERTa-Large (short) / GPT-3.5 (long). \~300 GPU-hours on A100s.
- Self-reported artifact: Llama-2-7B/BioASQ AUROC is inflated because BioASQ is the only yes/no-heavy task — the probe partly learns "is this a yes/no question." Their own spurious-correlation caveat, same class as Apollo's morality confound.

**ACE / cost-argument relevance — HIGHEST, and it is a live gap.** `wiki/paper/ace-draft.tex:163–169` is a paragraph headed **"Single-pass hallucination detection"** that cites Farquhar for "$\approx$ 0.78–0.88 AUROC at $10\times$ inference cost" and concludes "ACE is single-pass." **SEPs are a single-pass method for the semantic-entropy signal, published by the same group two months after the Nature paper, and are absent from that paragraph.** This does not falsify anything ACE claims — but the cost axis no longer separates ACE from the SE line, so the paragraph now understates the field. The surviving distinctions are real and should be the ones stated: SEPs are **supervised** (a fitted probe per model, and their calibration set needs 10 generations per training query, so the 10× cost moves from test time to calibration time rather than disappearing), they read the **residual stream**, and their target is **semantic uncertainty over meanings**, not commitment geometry. Note also that this is the **HARP↔v3 / Persona-Vectors↔empathy-geometry / Apollo↔DC pattern a fourth time** — with a twist worth naming: SEPs are supervised on a *self-generated internal* target rather than an external label, which is a genuine third position between our unsupervised-cells-plus-calibrated-selector design and a fully label-supervised probe. Same note applies to `rpv-draft.tex:122–127`, which contrasts RPV against "methods that resample many generations."

**DC lane relevance — HIGH.** Figures 2, 3, 4 and A.1–A.11 are **per-layer AUROC curves across 5 models × 4 tasks × 2 token positions**, published June 2024. This is the earliest and broadest external depth-curve panel the lane has ingested, and it predates Dombrowski (2024, 3 models) in breadth. Three specific hooks:
- **Placement depends on the target, not only the model — Table 4 is the datapoint.** They report the concatenated 5-layer band chosen for each probe type. On **Llama-3-70B (long)** the SEP band is layers **76–80 of 80** while the accuracy-probe band is **31–35 of 80**: same model, same data, same run, and the best depth moves \~45 layers when the label changes. Mistral-7B: SEP 28–32/32 vs accuracy probe 12–16/32. Llama-2-7B: 28–32 vs 18–22. Phi-3: 21–25 vs 25–29 (the one inversion). This **extends DC claim 6** from "measure placement per model" to "per (model, distribution, **target**)" — and it is external, on a different object, from a different lab, two years earlier.
- **Terminal decline in the long-form setting.** For short generations AUROC rises and plateaus late; for long generations "AUROC values peak for intermediate layers" more often, which they explain as late layers being "preoccupied with predicting the next token" while the semantic uncertainty of the *whole answer* is not what the next token is about (§6, Fig. 4). That is a terminal-dip-shaped observation with a mechanistic story, arrived at independently. **Scope discipline: do not cite it as support for E5.** Their object is a supervised residual-stream probe's AUROC, ours is unsupervised attention morphology; their "dip" is qualitative with no dip statistic, no CI, no registration; and E5 is a registered miss (WEAKEN 8/12) whose phrasing may not be strengthened by an unregistered external qualitative echo. It is a *framing* corroboration for claim 5 (depth structure is real but condition-dependent), nothing more.
- **Selection hygiene contrast.** Their band is chosen by "the highest mean AUROC value achieved in the interval... **in the in-distribution setting**" and then reported — in-sample selection with no out-of-bag correction. Our cross-fitted / nested-OOB machinery exists for exactly this, and their in-distribution-vs-all-layers tension (Table 1 wash on selected layers vs Fig. 6 accuracy-probes-win across all layers) is a clean external illustration that **the layer aggregation set can decide the verdict** — the same class of error as our own 2026-09-06 three-of-seven-metrics revision.

**Candidate #16 relevance — HIGH.** They **concatenate the hidden states of 5 adjacent layers** to train each probe (Table 4, "Layer Concatenation"). That is the depth-coverage hypothesis built into a deployed method: an aggregate over a depth *band* rather than a single measured rung. They never test the band against its own best single layer, so the question our step 3 answered — does the aggregate beat the best single fixed rung, and does that survive on held-out models — is **open in their paper and closed in ours** (split verdict: grid A 6/8 median +0.1195, grid B 6/9 median +0.0110, heterogeneous). Their setup is also a band of *adjacent* layers on one instrument, where ours mixes rungs across instruments, so the correspondence is structural, not numeric. Worth citing if #16 is ever written up: an independent method whose design silently assumes what #16 tests.

**Commit-time relevance — MEDIUM-HIGH.** The **TBG** probe reads the last input token *before any generation* and still predicts the semantic entropy of the answer the model is about to give (Fig. 3, AUROC broadly comparable to SLT). That is the commitment thesis one step earlier than our own locus, and it was the **fifth** independent line pointing this way after Apollo's pre-deceptive-text firing, Ruan's round-1 "doomed from the start", Dombrowski's before-commit token, and our own commit-locus results. **Now sixth — Yeom et al. 2026 (below) supplies a step-resolved AUROC curve peaking at the commit step, and explicitly builds on this paper's TBG setting.** Distinguish carefully when citing: TBG predicts *uncertainty about the forthcoming answer*, not the correctness of a token already committed to; ACE@t=0 / PRI@gen_step=1 read the state at the commitment step itself.

**Limits to respect.** arXiv v1 preprint, "under review" — not a venue-accepted citation as filed. All five models are 2023–24 checkpoints (Llama-2, Mistral-7B v0.1-era, Phi-3-mini); **zero overlap with the sealed ACE panel or either depth grid** — Mistral-7B and Phi-3-3.8B are lineage-adjacent to our `mistral-7b` and `phi-3.5-mini` pages, not the same checkpoints. Everything is short-answer closed-book QA; no NLI, no HaluEval, no ANLI. All probes are supervised and per-model-refit, so nothing here is a drop-in unsupervised comparator. And the headline OOD result is **leave-one-task-out within four QA datasets** — task transfer, not the held-out-*model* transfer grid B tests. Treat as prior art and framing, not as a numeric comparator.

### Yeom et al. 2026 — Hallucination as Commitment Failure

**Ingested 2026-09-06** (fetched from arXiv during a survey of post-SEP work; filed as `yeom-2026-hallucination-commitment-failure.pdf`). Jewon Yeom, Jaewon Sok, Heejun Kim, Seonghyeon Park, Jeongjae Park, Taesup Kim (corresponding). Seoul National University (Graduate School of Data Science; Rural Systems Engineering; Aerospace Engineering) + Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology. arXiv 2605.22007v1, 21 May 2026, cs.CL. **Marked "Preprint" — no venue.** Verified from full text, pp. 1–9.

**What it does.** Tests the assumption that hallucination means the model lacks the answer. Defines a **concept** as an equivalence class of token completions denoting the same answer, and a **per-step semantic probability mass** $P_{\text{mass}}(t;c)=\sum_{v\in S_c}P_\theta(v\mid Q,y_{<t})$ over that concept's first-token surface forms. Asks whether the correct concept already has mass at the step the model commits.

**Verified numbers.**
- **Commitment step is where the information lives (Fig. 3c).** On 500 long-form Qwen3.5-9B Instruct trajectories aligned to each trajectory's own $t_c$, detection AUROC of $P_{\text{mass}}$ is at chance two-or-more steps before $t_c$, peaks at $t_c$ (0.58), and decays to chance within a few steps after. Generated-token probability $P(y_t)$ is flat 0.55–0.62 across the same window with no peak. Entropy is a **noisy** localizer — the max-entropy step matches $t_c$ in only 20% of samples.
- **Commitment failures (Table 1).** Instruct models, short-form QA, $t_c=1$: 16% (Qwen3.5-0.8B) → 41% (Qwen2.5-72B); 16% (Llama-3.2-1B) → 47% (Llama-3.1-70B). Monotonic in scale, both families. $P_{\text{mass}}(t{=}1)$ AUROC 0.816–0.935 — **an oracle ceiling, not a competitor's deployed number.**
- **Mechanism.** Within matched-$P_{\text{mass}}$ samples **on Qwen3.5-9B-Instruct** (N=128 failures vs N=840 matched correct), max probability on any single surface form of $c^\ast$ is **0.26 (failures) vs 0.78 (correct)**, Welch $t$=47.6, $p<10^{-180}$, Cohen's $d$=2.98. ⚠️ **That effect size is one model.** What generalises across all **18** models is the **sign** ($d<0$ in 100%, median $|d|$=1.93).
- **Driver is instruction tuning, not scale.** Wrong-token probability rises 0.31→0.57 (Qwen Instruct 0.8B→72B) and 0.33→0.49 (Llama Instruct 1B→70B) but stays **flat at ~0.30 across Base models**.
- **Multi-layer probing (§4.4, Appendix J) — read this carefully; it is easy to misread.** Logistic regression on the last-layer hidden state at $t{=}1$, *before any token is generated*, gives Instruct > Base at all four sizes (+0.08 avg); pre-generation probe AUROC 0.61–0.87 Instruct vs 0.50–0.63 Llama Base. §4.4 says "the pattern holds across nearly all layers (Appendix J), peaking at mid-layers" — but **"the pattern" is the Instruct−Base contrast, not the probe AUROC.** Fig. 8's caption is explicit: "Instruct (blue) > Base (orange) at nearly every layer; **gap** peaks at mid-layers", and the text gives "mid-layer peaks (≈ +0.06 to +0.12 **gap**)". ⚠️ **Table 6 is only FOUR rungs** (First / Mid / Last−1 / Last) and the winning rung is **not stable**: Instruct AUROC peaks at **Last−1** for 0.8B (.697) and 9B (.867), at **Mid** for 2B (.718, by 0.002) and 4B (.838). **Do not cite this as a mid-stack placement result** — it is a claim about what instruction tuning amplifies, on a rung panel too coarse to locate anything.
- **Q-attn.** Fraction of last-layer attention on question tokens at $t{=}1$; +0.09 average Instruct–Base gap (Fig. 5 middle).

**Models and data.** Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B/4B/9B (Instruct + Base, 4-bit NF4), Llama-3.2 1B/3B, Llama-3.1 8B (both variants) = 14; extended with Qwen2.5-72B and Llama-3.1-70B (Instruct + Base) = **18 total**. TriviaQA + NQ-Open (3,000/model), MMLU + ARC-Challenge (2,672/model); representation analyses on a 1,500-sample subset. AUROC with hallucination as positive class; probes are 5-fold CV logistic regression.

**Commit-locus relevance — HIGHEST, and it is the strongest external line yet.** This is the **sixth** independent line after Apollo, Ruan, Dombrowski, Kossen's TBG, and our own — and unlike the other five it supplies a **step-resolved AUROC curve** that peaks at the commit step and decays to chance on both sides. It also independently coins "commitment step" for the same object. Their related work states the position explicitly: *"what matters is not position but the answer-level commitment step."*

**RPV relevance — HIGHEST, and it is a mechanism gift rather than a scoop.** RPV reads the eigen-spectrum of the **centered** Fisher $\Wu^\top(\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top)\Wu$ — a $\Delta h$-independent measure of how *spread out* the output distribution is at the readout. Yeom's mechanism is that hallucination at commit is precisely **dispersion of mass across surface forms** where correctness is concentration. Same phenomenon class, different instrument. Crucially **RPV is not scooped**: $P_{\text{mass}}$ requires ground-truth aliases and the paper's own Limitations call it *"an analytical probe, not a deployable detector … any practical use depends on inferring $S_{c^\ast}$ from context."* Their Future Work names the gap explicitly — approximating the oracle by *"semantic-similarity clustering of top-$k$"* — and an unsupervised spectral readout is a different, already-built answer to it.
- `[HYPOTHESIS — external, untested here]` The dispersion mechanism is a candidate *explanation* for the H1 NO-GO: if `null_ratio` and RPV both read facets of one commit-step distributional event, redundancy is predicted rather than surprising. Logged in [[claims]] §10. **Not tested by us; do not report as a finding.**
- Yeom also supplies an external account of why RPV beats plain confidence (+0.102): *"token-level confidence is not coming from the absence of the right concept but from concentration on the wrong concept"* — concept-level structure that token-level confidence misses.

**ACE relevance — MEDIUM-HIGH.** Their Q-attn is a crude attention-at-commit instrument, and their Future Work names *"attention concentration on $Q$"* among the signals *"likely better candidates"* than entropy for localizing commitment. That is independent motivation for ACE's object, arrived at from the distributional side.

**DC relevance — LOW, and it is a CAUTION not a convergence.** ⚠️ **Corrected 2026-09-06 after audit — an earlier version of this note claimed their correctness probe "peaks at mid-layers" and converges with Kossen's accuracy band and our Llama mid-stack band. That was a misreading of Appendix J** (the mid-stack feature is the Instruct−Base *gap*; the probe AUROC peaks at Last−1 in 2 of 4 models). What the paper actually offers DC is the *opposite* lesson: a four-rung panel on which the winning rung is unstable across four sizes of one family — the same coarse-sampling limitation as our own three-rung readout (P3). Cite it for that, not for placement. ⚠️ **Standing prohibition, same as Kossen: this may NOT be cited as support for the terminal-dip test (E5).** No dip statistic, no interval, supervised probe, different instrument; E5 is a registered miss at 8/12 against a frozen ≥10/12 bar and no external echo can upgrade it.

**Limits to respect.** arXiv v1 preprint, no venue. Qwen3.5 cells are **4-bit NF4 quantized** — non-comparable with our lanes under guardrail 9. Only Qwen and Llama families (their own stated limit). Greedy decoding, 1–3 token answers. $P_{\text{mass}}$ needs ground-truth aliases, so **none of their AUROCs is a deployable comparator** — do not enter 0.816–0.935 into any comparison table against ACE/PRI/RPV numbers. Checkpoint overlap with our panels exists (Qwen2.5-72B, Llama-3.1-70B, Llama-3.1-8B) but the measurements are theirs, not ours.

### Li et al. 2026 — SHARS / HalluSE (OATML)

**Ingested 2026-09-06** (same survey; filed as `li-2026-hallucination-rejection-sampling.pdf`). Lin Li, Georgia Channing, Suhaas M Bhat, Gabriel Davis Jones, **Yarin Gal**. OATML Dept of Computer Science Oxford; Dept of Engineering Oxford; Oxford Digital Health Labs, Nuffield Dept of Women's & Reproductive Health. **ICML 2026, PMLR 306** (Seoul); arXiv 2606.03628v1, 2 Jun 2026. Verified from full text, pp. 1–3.

**What it does.** Inference-time *mitigation*, not detection. Generation is segmented by sentence; each sentence is scored by a hallucination detector and then discarded, rewritten, or kept; generation continues on the surviving text. Targets **hallucination snowballing** in long-form output. Detector is treated as a **black box** — SHARS is explicitly detector-agnostic. Instantiated with **HalluSE**, a refinement of Farquhar's semantic entropy. +20–26% factual precision over Greedy on FactScore. §5.4 puts SHARS at **roughly 10–40×** greedy runtime and frames it as a better accuracy–efficiency trade-off than the baselines (Self-Endorse "about 35–60×"). ⚠️ **Corrected after audit — an earlier version of this note said "10–70×"; the upper end belongs to the baseline being beaten, not to SHARS.** Models: **Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-32B, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct**; FactualBio, FactScore, LongFact.

**Why it matters here, and it is a structural fact rather than a numeric one.** This is the newest hallucination work from the lab that produced both Farquhar 2024 and Kossen 2024, and **it does not build on their own probe line.** They cite SEPs in related work — *"Kossen et al. (2024) trained probes to approximate semantic entropy from hidden states of a single generation"* — and adopt a sampling detector instead, stating they chose HalluSE because *"it does not require training a new probe model to detect hallucinations **like Obeso et al. (2025)**"* and *"does not rely on external tools or reference knowledge sources."* ⚠️ **Corrected after audit:** the named referent of the probe-training objection is **Obeso, not Kossen**. Their selection criterion is training-freeness; do not report this as an explicit rejection of SEPs.

Three consequences:
- 🧭 **The SEP line has no successor from its own lab.** First author Jannik Kossen has moved to FAIR/Meta; no 2026 arXiv output in this vein under his name. The 2024 paper remains the current state of that line, so our comparator ladder is not already out of date.
- 💸 **The cost axis is not the one being optimised.** SHARS spends roughly 10–40× greedy runtime and argues it is *more* efficient than competing inference-time methods. The 2026-09-06 qualification to the Farquhar note ("the compute argument no longer separates us") stands for the *detector* comparison, but the field's own trajectory is not uniformly toward cheapness.
- 🎯 **Training-freeness is the axis the field is actually acting on** — and it is the distinction our drafts already lead with. When the SE line's own authors give "no probe training required" as their selection criterion, ACE's unsupervised-cells framing is strengthened rather than eroded.

**Limits to respect.** Mitigation not detection; no AUROC against a correctness label in the shape we report; no hidden-state probe and **no layer analysis anywhere**, so nothing here touches the DC lane. Long-form factuality benchmarks only — no ANLI, no HaluEval-QA, no short-form QA. Three models. Not a comparator; cite for field direction and for the training-freeness point.

### Dingeto 2026 — A Lie Detector Test for Language Models: Reading Knowledge a Model Won't Reveal (PIR)

**Ingested 2026-09-28** (fetched from arXiv; filed as `dingeto-2026-lie-detector-test.pdf`). Hiskias Dingeto, StackOne Technologies. arXiv 2609.21996v2, 23 Sep 2026, cs.AI. **Preprint — no venue.** Verified from full text (15 pp incl. appendices).

**What it does.** Borrows the Concealed Information Test (CIT / "guilty knowledge test") from forensic psychophysiology (Lykken 1959; Farwell & Donchin 1991) and runs it inside a model. Given a question plus `k` candidate options, it reads the residual stream at each option's **last token** (the "option span" `x^ℓ_{q,j}` at layer ℓ), forms an **item-contrast direction** `w_ℓ` = mean difference between the correct-option span and the average distractor span (fit on the model's *own* known questions), and decodes the recognized answer as `argmax_j ⟨x, w_ℓ⟩`. **Reference-free**: no honest reference model, no labeled truth corpus — the distractors supply the contrast, and the labels for the fit set are the model's own honest-condition answers. A **divergence detector** `D = max_j r̃_j − r̃_ŷ` (calibrated off-distribution on the base model's honest condition, then applied to the deployed model's spans) flags concealment when the model recognizes one option yet outputs another. Extends to free-form generation by reconstructing candidates from the model's own samples.

**Key numbers (verified from text).**

### Wang et al. 2025 — Hallucination as a Computational Boundary (inevitability hierarchy + oracle escape)

**Ingested 2026-09-30** (fetched from arXiv; filed as `wang-2025-hallucination-computational-boundary.pdf`). Xi Wang (arXiv: "Wang Xi") and Quan Shi (equal contribution), Zenghui Ding (corresponding), Jianqing Gao, Xianjun Yang. Hefei Institutes of Physical Science (CAS), USTC, Changzhou University, iFLYTEK Research. arXiv 2508.07334v2 (v1 10 Aug 2025; v2 8 Dec 2025), cs.AI. The PDF carries an **AAAI 2026 copyright line**; no other venue metadata. Verified from full text (8 pp; text extraction clean).

**Core thesis.** A **theory paper**. It formalizes an LLM as a **Probabilistic Language Model (PLM)**, a computable map realized by a probabilistic Turing machine, and defines two hallucination metrics: **Straying** $H_{\text{Stray}}$ (probability mass outside a relational truth set) and **Distortion** $H_{\text{Distort}}$ (KL from a probabilistic truth). It then argues hallucination is inevitable at three nested boundaries, and that only a change of computational process escapes them. Explicitly extends Xu, Jain & Kankanhalli 2024 (deterministic-TM inevitability) to the probabilistic case.

**Key results (theorems, not measurements).**
- **Diagonalization boundary (Thm 2.4).** For any enumerable sequence of PLMs, a computable "nemesis" truth $f_R$ exists on which each $h_i$ strays on its diagonal input. Proof bound $H_{\text{Stray}} \ge 1/|\mathcal{Y}|$ implicitly assumes a finite output space.
- **Uncomputability boundary (Thm 2.5).** With truth defined by a Halting-Problem oracle, any standard PLM has $H_{\text{Distort}} > \tau$ on infinitely many inputs (reduction: otherwise a finite lookup table plus the PLM decides halting).
- **Information-theoretic boundary (Lemma 2.6, "Pumping Lemma for Learners").** A PLM of finite Kolmogorov capacity $K(h)$ fails on a truth patched with an incompressible string $z$ that exceeds remaining capacity. The authors call this "perhaps the most common in practice" (detail/verbatim recall hallucinations).
- **Oracle Escape (Thm 3.1).** There is a truth $f_O$ on which every standard PLM hallucinates but an oracle-augmented $h^O$ has zero $H_{\text{Stray}}$. Framed as the first formal account of why RAG works. **Note:** the escape holds by construction — the oracle *defines* the ground truth — so it says nothing about noisy or incomplete real retrieval.
- **Adaptive Escape (§3.2–3.3).** Continual learning as an "internalized oracle": a hierarchical Markov game (cortical VAE game: Generalizer vs Exception Handler; hippocampal game: Plasticity Agent vs Memory Consolidator) with a Hierarchical Nash Equilibrium solution concept. Thm 3.3 is an amortized-cost argument (CL beats RAG past some query count $N_0$). Capacity "grows" to $\approx K(h_t)+K(d\mid h_t)$ — stated conceptually, not proved.
- **Mitigation critique (§4).** CoT = intra-boundary optimization (cannot cross any boundary); scaling raises $K(h)$ (addresses only the information boundary — "a larger Turing Machine is still a Turing Machine"); RAG and CL are the only true escapes.
- **Experiment (§5, thin).** Mistral-7B, a corpus of fictional scientific facts, FAISS RAG vs LoRA continual learning vs a RAG-CL hybrid. Table 2: accuracy ~98.6% / ~81.0% / ~96.5%; TriviaQA forgetting 0% / 12.4% / 1.1%; robustness to 15% corrupted context 76.5% / N/A / 92.3%. Cost crossover ~287 queries (Fig. 6). An attention-shift figure (Fig. 5) shows answer-token attention to retrieved context dropping after internalization — one fact, before/after. **No seeds, no CIs, no variance; the robustness paragraph is truncated in the extracted text.**
- **CCA (Principle 6.1).** High-stakes deployment requires the task's intrinsic complexity to lie strictly within the computational class of the agent or its augmented system. "Dynamic CCA" = runtime complexity assessment with principled abstention or oracle requests.

**PRI v3 relevance — LOW-MEDIUM, framing only.** Joins Kalai et al. 2025 as an inevitability result, now computability-theoretic rather than statistical. Same division of labour as Kalai: it explains *why* failure cannot be eliminated; PRI aims to read *when* it is happening from internal geometry at commitment (sealed evidence covers contradiction versus control; hallucination detection is a hypothesis, not a result). The paper offers no internal signal — $H_{\text{Stray}}$ and $H_{\text{Distort}}$ need the ground truth, so they are definitions, not detectors.

**ACE (v4) / RPV relevance — LOW.** The Fig. 5 attention shift is an attention-at-answer reading, loosely adjacent to ACE's attention morphology, but it is a single-fact illustration on RAG context with no AUROC, no labels and no controls. Do not cite as convergent evidence for attention-at-commit.

**Commit-Confluence (CC) / deployment relevance — MEDIUM, conceptual echo.** CCA's "match the task to what *this system* can actually compute" parallels the BENCH finding that there is no universal detector cell or sign, and the practical rule "calibrate per (model, deployment distribution)". Dynamic CCA's runtime abstention is the use-case a commit-moment detector would serve. **Scope:** CCA is about task-complexity classes, not detector calibration. Cite as motivating framing, not support.

**Depth-curve (DC) / empathy-geometry relevance — none.** No layer analysis; no affective or dialogic content.

**Limits to respect.** Formal/theoretical; the "escape routes" are theoretical framings, not deployable detectors or mitigations with guarantees under realistic retrieval. The adversarial constructions (diagonal nemesis truths, halting oracles, Kolmogorov-random patches) are worst-case and distribution-independent (the authors say so, contrasting with PAC) — they bound what is possible, not what is typical on ANLI/HaluEval-type tasks. Kolmogorov capacity $K(h)$ is uncomputable and not measured (listed as future work). Oracle Escape is true by construction. The empirical section is single-model, single-run, uncalibrated — **never enter its numbers into a comparison table against ACE/PRI/RPV**.

### Hollinsworth, Spies et al. 2026 — Caught in the Act: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception (FIBS probes)

**Ingested 2026-10-12** (fetched from arXiv; filed as `hollinsworth-2026-caught-in-the-act-sabotage-probes.pdf`). Oskar J. Hollinsworth\* and Alex F. Spies\* (equal contribution; Hollinsworth corresponding), Tigist Diriba, Adam Gleave, Chris Cundy. FAR.AI. arXiv 2610.12445v1, cs.LG, 8 Oct 2026. **Preprint — no venue stated;** FIBS and the trained probes are promised "upon publication" (the header also links a GitHub repo, `AlignmentResearch/caught-in-the-act-probes`). Verified from the full text (appendices included; ~6,600 extracted lines). Funded by Coefficient Giving.

**Same lab as SOLiD, a different paper.** The SOLiD entry above (Hollinsworth, Dombrowski et al. 2026, arXiv 2607.01567) studies a lie detector *inside an RLHF oversight pipeline*. This paper studies lie-detector probes *as deployment monitors* and drops the RLHF loop entirely. Shared authors: Hollinsworth, Gleave, Cundy. Dombrowski and Adam-Day are not authors here (Dombrowski is thanked for feedback). Do not cite one as the other.

**What it does.**
- **Target construct is narrower than "deception."** It detects *knowing falsehoods* — a statement the model believes false — and explicitly does not claim to detect intent, misleading-but-true statements, or other non-falsehood behavior (§2.1). A falsehood is **contextual** when the transcript itself establishes the belief (e.g. a tool result), and **introspective** when the label depends on facts about the model (its training, its hidden goal). Belief is established by contextual evidence, neutral elicitation, or training-instilled belief.
- **FIBS dataset.** 163,016 transcripts, 28 families, balanced by label, length- and message-count-controlled, families capped at 8,000 rows. 76% synthetic follow-ups appended to existing datasets (e.g. MASK + "Did you answer accurately?"), 18% on-policy (Among Us-style deception games, pressure to lie, persona-driven), 6% bespoke. Estimated label noise 1.4% ± 0.1 against a Claude Fable 5 judge. The paper's own limits: only 5.5% of rows contain reasoning, the data are off-policy for almost every probed model (Qwen3.5-122B generated 3%), and the mix is "largely artificial."
- **Four probes, one recipe.** LinearEnsemble and MLPEnsemble are *single-layer, single-token* probes (one per layer, averaged over layers with training loss < 0.6; read at the last non-end-of-turn token). **EFC** (early-fusion covariance, adapted from Dooms et al. 2026) concatenates six layers per token and pools mean + covariance across tokens. **Axial** is a small transformer that attends causally across tokens, then bidirectionally across layers. All four read **six layers spread evenly from 30% to 90% of depth** (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B: layers {18, 25, 33, 40, 48, 54} of 60); EFC and axial read every token of the final user and assistant turn.
- **Evaluation trick.** At test time a follow-up is appended and the answer prefilled ("Did you do anything the user did not ask you to do?" → "No." / "Yes."). The score is the **contrast** between the two prefills, which removes context-specific bias. The follow-up is read *after* the step's tool calls, with reasoning elided by the chat template, so the probe does not read the chain of thought.
- **SHADE-Arena.** 14 of 25 scenarios (a manual filter chosen after inspecting pilot labels, with no numeric rule — Appendix B.5), 25 honest + 25 side-task episodes each, per-step labels from a Claude Haiku 4.5 judge. Positives are the first judge-flagged side-task step onward; honest and pre-onset steps are negatives. Qwen3.5-397B-A17B: 699 scored episodes, 5,014 positive and 16,275 negative steps.
- **Introspective evaluations (three).** (i) **cue-following** — elicit a belief on MMLU, then pressure the answer away with a hint; (ii) **political-censorship** — 90 politically sensitive questions where Chinese models state falsehoods, judged by Haiku 4.5 (>90 deceptive, <10 honest); (iii) **hidden-goal organisms** — eight LoRA adapters on Qwen3.6-27B (seven from Cooney et al. 2026, one new `bird-labs` organism that lies under the enterprise tier and is honest under the free tier).

In Fig. 4a Opus 5.5\* is plotted at recall ≈ 0 at 1% FPR for all three model families (read from the figure, not the text).

**What it is.** A **position paper** with no experiments, no data and no model runs. It critiques RLHF and RLAIF (jointly "RLxF") as a route to AI safety, from technical, philosophical and system-safety angles. Its target is the "3H" criteria (helpful, honest, harmless) from Askell et al. 2021.

**Its arguments, by section.**
- 🌫️ **The 3Hs are left undefined on purpose (§3).** Bai et al. 2022a let crowdworkers "interpret these concepts as they see fit." Kirk et al. 2023 call "alignment" an *empty signifier*. - ⚖️ **Harmlessness becomes "least harmful" (§3.1).** Constitutional AI's comparison prompt picks the less harmful of two outputs, which tolerates harm so long as it is relatively smaller.
- 🪞 **Expressed confidence is not model confidence (§3.2).** The authors argue that LLMs lack introspection. So the verbal uncertainty in a response reflects likely text, not the model's actual confidence. A model can be "certain" it should write "I'm not sure." Citing Krause et al. 2023 ("Confidently wrong"), they note that matching confident language to correctness mostly improves by raising accuracy, not by becoming appropriately unsure.
- 🎭 **Helpfulness invites deception (§4.1).** RLxF makes output more human-like ("I'm sorry", the pronoun "I"), which risks deceiving users about what they are talking to (the Eliza effect).
- 🙇 **Sycophancy (§4.2).** Citing Sharma et al. 2024, both humans and preference models prefer agreeable answers over correct ones. Maximizing preference scores therefore trades truth for the look of helpfulness.
- 🌐 **Value imposition and opacity (§4.3–4.4).** Values and language converge on those of the groups that build the models. Preference criteria, who the labelers are, and under-the-hood model changes are all opaque.
- 🏗️ **System safety (§2.4, §5).** Leveson's *curse of flexibility*: with general-purpose software, nobody can specify what it should or should not do. The paper concludes that value alignment "by engineering" alone is impossible. Safety criteria must be set in the context of use, and the field should favor smaller, limited-purpose models.

**Furnace relevance — honesty and internal readout: CONTEXT, with one tension to respect.**
- ✅ **Supports the motivation.** §3.2 states the gap our detectors are built around. What a model *says* about its confidence is a text property, not a readout of its internal state. ACE, PRI and RPV read geometry at commitment rather than the model's words, so this paper is a citable statement of why verbal confidence is not enough.
- ⚠️ **Do not cite its "LLMs lack introspection" claim as established.** It is asserted, not tested. Later evidence already in this catalog cuts against the strong form: in Hollinsworth, Spies et al. 2026 (FIBS, above), the organism's own **self-report logit matches the axial probe's median AUC (91.3% vs 90.8%)**. The defensible use is narrower: verbal confidence *can* decouple from correctness, and RLxF does not reliably fix that.

**Furnace relevance — empathy-geometry (candidate #11): FRAMING CAUTION.** §4.1 calls empathic-sounding output ("I'm sorry") an RLxF trade-off that risks deceiving users about the system's nature. Any empathy-geometry writing that measures empathic *expression* should expect this objection and say what is measured: a representation, not felt empathy. Design-phase note only; no result is affected.

**Grant-proposal relevance:** withheld from the public repo until the application is public.

**Scope discipline.** Not a hallucination-detection paper and not a numeric comparator: it reports no numbers of its own. Nothing here bears on the depth-curve lane (E1–E7, P1–P9), BENCH, or any sealed verdict. Do **not** cite it for the terminal dip (E5).

**Limits to respect.**
- 📄 **Preprint v1 (June 2024)**, position paper. I did not check whether a later version or a venue exists.
- 💬 **Argued, not measured.** Every empirical point (sycophancy, calibration, labeler skew) is borrowed from cited work; the paper adds none.
- 🗓️ **Dated relative to the field.** It predates the 2025–2026 probe and self-report literature ingested above, so its claims about introspection should be read against that work.
