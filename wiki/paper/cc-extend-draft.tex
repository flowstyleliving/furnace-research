\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The Commit-Confluence study established that decoder-LLM hallucination has \emph{no universal
detector but a universal floor}: a nested out-of-bag selector, fit per deployment, clears chance
on almost every (model, task) cell, yet no single signal wins everywhere. This companion asks the
next question the sealed run could not: does that floor survive when we move to a new hallucination
family---grounded source-faithfulness judgment (HaluEval-QA)---and, crucially, can a \emph{fixed}
detector be shipped, or must each model still be calibrated on its own labels? We pre-registered a
two-part conjunction. \textbf{A1} asks whether each of ten models is individually deployable on
HaluEval-QA under its own calibration; it \textbf{passes $10/10$}. \textbf{A2} asks the stronger
question---freeze one cell and one orientation, fit that orientation on nine models, and apply it
blind to the tenth---and it \textbf{fails $6/10$}. The conjunction is therefore not satisfied, and
the reason is sharp: the four failures are not weak signals but \emph{sign inversions}, models on
which the frozen orientation is confidently backwards (AUROC as low as $0.174$). The floor extends
to HaluEval-QA, but only in per-model-calibrated form; a transferable fixed-orientation detector
does not exist across this cohort. We report the result under strict pre-registration discipline,
including an enforced checksum provenance chain over every published matrix, and we are explicit
about what the run does and does not license.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why an extension, and what it fixes}

The sealed Commit-Confluence run scored two benchmarks (ANLI~R1 and TriviaQA-paired) across ten
open-weight models and reached a two-part reading: the per-deployment selector is nearly always
deployable, but no single commit-moment signal is universally best. Two questions were left open.
First, both benchmarks are \emph{entailment/answer-correctness} tasks; whether the floor reaches a
different hallucination family---judging whether a candidate answer is \emph{faithful to supplied
source knowledge}---was untested. Second, and more consequentially for anyone who wants to deploy a
monitor, the sealed run only ever calibrated \emph{per model}. It never asked whether a single
frozen detector could be shipped to a model it was not tuned on. A universal floor under per-model
calibration is a scientific statement; a transferable fixed detector is a product. They are not the
same claim, and the sealed run could not separate them.

The extension (``CC extension'', BENCH) adds six tasks---HaluEval QA / dialogue / summarization,
ANLI~R2, and replications---and pre-registers a conjunction that forces the calibration-versus-transfer
distinction into the open. We report the HaluEval-QA endpoints here.

\paragraph{Two repairs, disclosed.} Two defects were found and fixed \emph{before} any strict cell
completed, and both are disclosed because they affect how the analyses are read. (i)~The transfer
estimator carried a hard-coded input-version string that lagged an earlier normalizer amendment; it
was synchronized to the current spec and then replaced by a shared dependency-free constant. The
repair predates every registered metric and changed neither the estimator nor the bar. (ii)~The
label-efficiency subsampler drew independent rows; on paired tasks this can split a question stem
from its twin. It was corrected prospectively to draw complete stems on grouped tasks, with ungrouped
tasks retaining row draws.

\section{The pre-registered conjunction}

Both endpoints score the same ten models on HaluEval-QA ($n{=}1000$; $500$ faithful, $500$
hallucinated; balanced), using the same $29$-signal panel and the same nested-OOB selector as the
seal. The bootstrap unit is the \emph{question stem}, not the row---the conservative choice on paired
data. Both endpoints carry a registered bar of $\geq 8/10$.

\section{A1 passes: the floor reaches HaluEval-QA under calibration}

Under per-model calibration, all ten models are deployable: A1 is $10/10$ against its $\geq 8/10$
bar. Every model clears its controls, no cell drops a sample, and no model emits a non-canonical
commitment on HaluEval-QA. The cohort's \emph{weakest} stem-cluster geometric OOB lower bound is
$0.6705$ (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}); the rest sit comfortably higher. This licenses a narrow, honest
statement: the commit-moment floor extends to grounded source-faithfulness judgment \emph{when each
model may use its own labeled calibration}.

Notably, A1 selects \emph{eight distinct winning cells} across the ten models. There is already no
universal \emph{best} cell here---the very pattern the sealed run reported on the original two
benchmarks, now reproduced on a third hallucination family.

\section{A2 fails: orientation does not transfer}

The stronger endpoint fails. With the cell and sign frozen and applied blind, A2 clears the bar on
only $6/10$ holdouts (\texttt{aborted=false}). The pre-registered A1$\wedge$A2 conjunction is
therefore \textbf{not satisfied}: the floor extends to HaluEval-QA in per-model-calibrated form, and
the run does \emph{not} license a transferable fixed detector across this cohort.

\paragraph{The failure is a sign inversion, not an absence of signal.} The four misses in
Table~\ref{tab:a2} do not cluster near $0.5$; they sit well below it. Each of these models, calibrated
on its \emph{own} labels (the A1 regime), selects the \emph{opposite} orientation ($+1$) from the six
that transfer ($-1$). Under the pooled $-1$ sign they are therefore read upside down. Reversing the
sign rescues each of them---$0.174\!\to\!0.826$, $0.206\!\to\!0.794$, $0.276\!\to\!0.724$,
$0.394\!\to\!0.606$---but \emph{reversal is not an A2 rescue}: choosing to reverse requires knowing the
held-out model's labels, which is exactly the information A1 may use and blind transfer forbids. We
verified the mechanism from the raw score matrices, not the summaries: rebuilding the fused column
per model with the production routine reproduces the registered AUROCs to three decimal places, and
the mean fused rank of faithful-versus-hallucinated examples mirrors exactly---e.g.\ Llama-3.2-3B
$0.62/0.38$ (high~$=$~faithful) against Mistral-7B $0.37/0.63$ (high~$=$~hallucinated).

\paragraph{Polarity is generation-split, not a family law.} The orientation is coherent within some
lineages and not others. Mistral (both members $+1$) and Llama (both $-1$) are family-coherent, but
Qwen and Phi \emph{split by generation}: Qwen2.5 inverts while Qwen3 holds, and Phi-3.5 inverts while
Phi-4 holds. This is descriptive only---one or two members per subgroup, with family confounded by
tokenizer, architecture, and size---but it is enough to rule out ``orientation is a property of the
model family.''

\section{What is and is not universal}

The result must be restated carefully, because it is easy to over- or under-claim.

\begin{itemize}
\item There is \textbf{no universal orientation}: the same cell reads faithfulness in opposite
directions across models, and no single sign serves all ten.
\item There is \textbf{no universal best cell}: A1 selects eight distinct winners across ten models.
\item But this is \textbf{not} the absence of a common informative cell. The frozen cell
\texttt{fusion\_rank\_mean\_geom} clears $0.55$ on \emph{all ten} models once each model's sign is
calibrated. A2 rejects the compound deployment claim ``\emph{fixed cell plus fixed sign},'' not the
identity of the cell.
\end{itemize}

The practical reading is blunt: a commit-moment hallucination monitor for grounded faithfulness can be
built for any of these ten models, but it needs a small labeled calibration set from \emph{that} model
to fix its orientation. There is no drop-in detector that transfers unseen. This sharpens rather than
contradicts the sealed thesis: the universal object is the \emph{fitting procedure}, not any fixed
signal, and now not even a fixed \emph{orientation}.

\section{Provenance and honest attestation}

The extension is published with an enforced checksum chain rather than a trust-me summary. A sidecar
manifest (\texttt{PROVENANCE.json}, schema \texttt{bench-provenance/1.1}) binds a SHA-256 for every
one of the $53$ score matrices and $53$ profiles, the run sidecars, and the lifecycle logs, together
with the expected counts and matrix-to-profile pairing. A verifier
(\texttt{verify\_bench\_provenance.py}, exercised in CI) recomputes the tree and fails closed on any
mismatch, unsafe path, or malformed manifest. We are deliberate about its scope: it establishes
\emph{repository coherence}---that the published files are mutually consistent and unmodified---not an
execution-time signature. Where a re-run was used to re-attest the closed result, it is recorded under
its own dated name; the original detached run's exit status was never captured and is documented as
unrecoverable rather than reconstructed after the fact.

\paragraph{Comparability.} All HaluEval-QA cells are reported within a single MLX extraction stack and
are byte-comparable with one another. They are \emph{not} pooled with the study's non-byte-comparable
extension cells (GPU/torch scale panels or reimplemented multimodal extractors); those live in
separate, clearly labeled sections of the parent study.

\section{A note on label cost}

The sealed study's label-efficiency curve is measured at budgets $n\in\{50,100,150\}$ only. Mean
geometric deployability rises $0.44\!\rightarrow\!0.66\!\rightarrow\!0.79$ across those budgets and is
\emph{still rising} at the largest one. We therefore report $150$ labels as a measured lower bound on
the calibration a per-deployment detector needs, not as a sufficiency threshold or a ``knee.'' The
A1 result above is consistent with this: per-model calibration is what makes HaluEval-QA deployable,
and calibration is not free.

\section{Forward work (pre-registered, not yet run)}

Two documents are prepared and sealed for future, blind execution; neither is applied retroactively to
the closed run. The first proposes a per-cell commitment error budget and a widened
acceptable-answer template with an explicit blip-versus-behavior split, to be pre-registered before any
fresh data is inspected. The second is a designed-retrospective screen---predictor frozen in
advance---for a suggestive coincidence: the three models whose orientation inverts here overlap the
partial-transfer cluster seen in an earlier sealed morphology run. That overlap is logged as an
observation, not a finding, and any causal claim is deferred to a fresh-data registered replication.

\paragraph{Bottom line.} On a third, independent hallucination family, the commit-moment floor holds
under calibration ($10/10$) but a fixed orientation does not transfer ($6/10$), and it fails by
confident inversion rather than by noise. The monitor is real; it is just not universal in the one way
a shippable product would need it to be.
