\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The Commit-Confluence study established that decoder-LLM hallucination has \emph{no universal
detector but a universal floor}: a nested out-of-bag selector, fit per deployment, clears chance
on almost every (model, task) cell, yet no single signal wins everywhere. This companion reports
the complete pre-registered BENCH extension of that study: three task families---confirmatory
breadth (HaluEval-QA), confirmatory fresh-draw evaluation (an ANLI~R1 re-test at $5\times n$ on
the train distribution, plus clustered paired TriviaQA), and exploratory robustness (ANLI~R2,
HaluEval dialogue and summarization)---across the same ten models and six tasks; the realized run
assigns a terminal status to all $60$ model--task slots. The headline is the breadth conjunction. \textbf{A1} asks
whether each of ten models is individually deployable on HaluEval-QA under its own calibration; it
\textbf{passes $10/10$}. \textbf{A2} asks the stronger question---freeze one cell and one
orientation, fit that orientation on nine models, and apply it blind to the tenth---and it
\textbf{fails $6/10$}. The conjunction is therefore not satisfied, and the reason is sharp: the
four failures are not weak signals but \emph{sign inversions}, models on which the frozen
orientation reads backwards (point AUROC as low as $0.174$ on $n{=}1000$). Because exactly one of
A1 and A2 passes, the frozen registration licenses the statement that the floor \emph{partially
extends} to HaluEval-QA, with A2 named as the failing endpoint: A1 supports per-model-calibrated
deployability, the one pre-registered fixed-cell-plus-fixed-sign scoring rule fails its transfer
bar in this cohort, and the run licenses no claim about fixed scoring rules it did not test. Both
replication endpoints \textbf{miss their registered bar} ($7/20$ against $\geq 17/20$); that FAIL
is the registered verdict. Descriptively, all $18$ replication profiles that exist are
geometrically deployable, and the loss from $20$ planned cells to the registered $7/20$ is
localized to the frozen commitment and systematic-abort rules. The registered B2 probe finds both
sealed ANLI orphans deployable on the fresh $n{=}1000$ train-distribution re-test, narrowing the
orphan reading by supporting a small-$n$ artifact component without erasing the sealed failures.
We report everything under the registration's frozen language rules; a post-run checksum manifest
verifies repository coherence for every published matrix (it is not an execution-time signature
and recomputes no endpoint), and we are explicit about what the run does and does not license.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why an extension, and what it fixes}

The sealed Commit-Confluence run~\cite{ccpaper} scored two benchmarks (ANLI~R1 and TriviaQA-paired) across ten
open-weight models and reached a two-part reading: the per-deployment selector is nearly always
deployable, but no single commit-moment signal is universally best. Two questions were left open.
First, both benchmarks are \emph{entailment/answer-correctness} tasks; whether the floor reaches a
different hallucination family---judging whether a candidate answer is \emph{faithful to supplied
source knowledge}---was untested. Second, and more consequentially for anyone who wants to deploy a
monitor, the sealed run only ever calibrated \emph{per model}. It never asked whether a single
frozen detector could be shipped to a model it was not tuned on. A universal floor under per-model
calibration is a scientific statement; a transferable fixed detector is a product. They are not the
same claim, and the sealed run could not separate them.

The extension (``CC extension'', BENCH) adds six tasks---HaluEval QA / dialogue /
summarization~\cite{halueval}, ANLI~R2~\cite{anli}, and fresh-draw re-tests of ANLI~R1 (at
$5\times n$ on the train distribution---by registration, \emph{not} a same-distribution
replication) and paired TriviaQA~\cite{triviaqa}---and pre-registers a conjunction that forces the
calibration-versus-transfer distinction into the open, two replication endpoints with their own
bar, and an orphan probe. This companion reports the complete extension: every registered endpoint
(A1, A2, B1-procedural, B1-valid, B2) and the exploratory breadth cells.

\paragraph{Two repairs, disclosed.} Two defects were found and fixed \emph{before} any strict cell
completed, and both are disclosed because they affect how the analyses are read. (i)~The transfer
estimator carried a hard-coded input-version string that lagged an earlier normalizer amendment; it
was synchronized to the current spec and then replaced by a shared dependency-free constant. The
repair predates every registered metric and changed neither the estimator nor the bar. (ii)~The
label-efficiency subsampler drew independent rows; on paired tasks this can split a question stem
from its twin. It was corrected prospectively to draw complete stems on grouped tasks, with ungrouped
tasks retaining row draws. Both repairs were filed as numbered amendments in the repository's
pre-registration lineage; the second touches only the label-efficiency analysis, which is descriptive
and non-gating, and neither alters the A1/A2 endpoint definitions or bars.

\section{A1 passes: the floor reaches HaluEval-QA under calibration}

Under per-model calibration, all ten models are deployable: A1 is $10/10$ against its $\geq 8/10$
bar. Every model clears its controls, no cell drops a sample, and no model emits a non-canonical
commitment on HaluEval-QA. The cohort's \emph{weakest} stem-cluster geometric OOB lower bound is
$0.6705$ (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}); the rest sit comfortably higher. A1 alone licenses only the
calibration half of the registered claim; under the frozen language rule the extension's overall
verdict is that the floor \emph{partially extends} to HaluEval-QA, with A2 (next section) named
as the failing endpoint. (Throughout this companion, ``floor'' in the A1 sense
means per-deployment-calibrated deployability; the parent study's fixed-fusion leave-one-model-out
floor is exactly the object A2 tests---and rejects---on this task.)

Notably, A1 selects \emph{eight distinct winning cells} across the ten models. There is already no
universal \emph{best} cell here---the very pattern the sealed run reported on the original two
benchmarks, now reproduced on a third hallucination family.

\section{A2 fails: orientation does not transfer}

The stronger endpoint fails. With the cell and sign frozen and applied blind, A2 clears the bar on
only $6/10$ holdouts (\texttt{aborted=false}). The pre-registered A1$\wedge$A2 conjunction is
therefore \textbf{not satisfied}. In the registration's frozen wording, the floor \emph{partially
extends} to HaluEval-QA, with A2 the failing endpoint: per-model calibration is licensed, and
shipping this frozen cell-and-sign to an uncalibrated model is not.

\paragraph{The failure is a sign inversion, not an absence of signal.} The four misses in
Table~\ref{tab:a2} do not cluster near $0.5$; they sit well below it. Each of these models, calibrated
on its \emph{own} labels (the A1 regime), selects the \emph{opposite} orientation ($+1$) from the six
that transfer ($-1$). Under the pooled $-1$ sign they are therefore read upside down. Reversing the
sign rescues each of them---$0.174\!\to\!0.826$, $0.206\!\to\!0.794$, $0.276\!\to\!0.724$,
$0.394\!\to\!0.606$---but \emph{reversal is not an A2 rescue}: choosing to reverse requires knowing the
held-out model's labels, which is exactly the information A1 may use and blind transfer forbids. We
verified the mechanism from the raw score matrices, not the summaries: rebuilding the fused column
per model with the production routine reproduces the registered AUROCs to three decimal places, and
the mean fused rank of faithful-versus-hallucinated examples mirrors exactly---e.g.\ Llama-3.2-3B
$0.62/0.38$ (high~$=$~faithful) against Mistral-7B $0.37/0.63$ (high~$=$~hallucinated).

\paragraph{Observed polarity varies within named families.} The orientation is coherent within some
lineages and not others. Mistral's two checkpoints share $+1$ and Llama's two share $-1$, while the
tested Qwen and Phi checkpoints differ across generations: Qwen2.5 inverts where both Qwen3 models
hold, and Phi-3.5 inverts where Phi-4 holds. This is descriptive only. With one or two
representatives per subgroup, and generation confounded with tokenizer, architecture, size, and
instruction tuning, the observation is incompatible only with a \emph{simple family-constant
orientation in this cohort}; it does not identify generation---or anything else---as the cause.

\section{B1: the replications miss their bar --- and exactly how}

The registered replication endpoints fail, and the registration requires this miss to be stated
prominently in any publication of the extension. \textbf{B1-procedural and B1-valid both score
$7/20$ against their $\geq 17/20$ bar.} Replication-strength language (``the sealed result
replicates on fresh data'') is therefore \emph{not} licensed, on either bootstrap unit.

The registered verdict is $7/20$ FAIL. The following decomposition is \emph{descriptive}: it
locates where planned cells are lost, and it does not modify or rescue that endpoint:

The registered count falls from $20$ planned cells to $7$ passes in three layers. First, two
\texttt{anli\_r1\_rep} cells are \emph{behavioral} failures: \texttt{Phi-3.5} emits a newline
preamble and \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B} emits the chain-of-thought opener (`` To'') instead of a
canonical YES/NO commitment. Per the registration's own disclosure rule we state plainly that the
commitment normalizer \emph{was amended between smokes and strict cells} (Amendment~A1): the
canonical-commit predicate became ``a non-empty prefix of \texttt{yes} or \texttt{no},'' which
rescues exactly one signature---\texttt{Mistral-7B}'s tokenizer splits \texttt{YES} into
\texttt{Y}+\texttt{ES}, so its first-token \texttt{Y} is a genuine commit misread by the old
exact-form rule---while the newline and `` To'' signatures were explicitly left as failures, with
no behavioral rescue. Those two failures leave $18$ emitted profiles. Second,
\texttt{Qwen3-8B/anli\_r1\_rep} and three \texttt{triviaqa\_paired\_rep} cells fail the
zero-error commitment audit (a single non-canonical commitment in $1000$ disqualifies a cell),
leaving $14/20$ admissible. Third, the three TriviaQA failures (rates $1/1000$, $1/1000$, and
$12/1000$---\texttt{gemma-3-4b} sometimes answers the trivia question instead of judging the
candidate) trigger the frozen systematic-abort rule, which scores all ten TriviaQA cells as
failures, including the seven whose terminal status is OK. The registered endpoint is therefore
$7/20$. The cascade was registered before the data existed and is applied as written; per the
registration's amendment discipline it cannot be softened retroactively, and a successor rule (a
per-cell error budget with a blip-versus-behavior split) exists only as a draft for a
\emph{future} registration (\S~\ref{sec:forward}).

Read precisely: $7/20$ is the registered B1 verdict. The $18/18$ geometric count is a descriptive
decomposition of the profiles that exist (Table~\ref{tab:b1}; Fig.~\ref{fig:panel}), not a
registered rescue endpoint. It shows that the observed loss occurs in behavioral admissibility
and the systematic-abort cascade rather than in the geometry---and it neither licenses
replication-strength language nor alters the FAIL.

\section{B2: both sealed orphans are deployable on the larger train-distribution re-test}

The sealed run left two non-deployable ANLI cells---\texttt{gemma-3-4b/anli} (geometric CI-lo
$0.403$) and \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B/anli}---and the extension registered a probe of those cells on
a fresh $n{=}1000$ draw, reported either way. As the registration itself requires us to say, this
is a \emph{re-test of the sealed construct at $5\times n$ on the noisier ANLI train
distribution}, not a same-distribution replication (the dev split could not supply $1000$ fresh
balanced rows). \textbf{Both pass:} \texttt{gemma-3-4b} reaches a geometric OOB CI lower bound of
$0.772$ and \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} $0.676$ on \texttt{anli\_r1\_rep}. Under the registered
reading, these passes \emph{narrow} the orphan interpretation by supporting a small-$n$ artifact
component; they do not erase the sealed failures or alter the sealed verdict, which stands as
sealed. The result is descriptively convergent with the parent study's separate scale-axis
closures, but because this comparison changes both sample size and ANLI split/distribution, it
does not isolate sample size as the cause.

\section{What is and is not universal}

The result must be restated carefully, because it is easy to over- or under-claim.

\begin{itemize}
\item There is \textbf{no universal orientation}: the same cell reads faithfulness in opposite
directions across models, and no single sign serves all ten.
\item There is \textbf{no universal best cell}: A1 selects eight distinct winners across ten models.
\item But this is \textbf{not} the absence of a common informative cell. The frozen cell
\texttt{fusion\_rank\_mean\_geom} clears $0.55$ on \emph{all ten} models once each model's sign is
calibrated. A2 rejects the compound deployment claim ``\emph{fixed cell plus fixed sign},'' not the
identity of the cell.
\end{itemize}

The practical reading is blunt: a commit-moment faithfulness monitor can be built for any of these
ten models, but it needs a labeled calibration set from \emph{that} model to fix its orientation
(how \emph{few} labels suffice on this task was not measured; see the label-cost note below). The
one drop-in detector this run pre-registered---the obvious candidate, the cell that is informative
on all ten---does not transfer unseen; the run tested no other fixed detector. This sharpens rather
than contradicts the sealed thesis: the universal object is the \emph{fitting procedure}, not any
fixed signal, and now not even a fixed \emph{orientation}.

\section{Scope, construct, and limitations}

Stated plainly, because a careful reader will ask.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Construct.} Every HaluEval-QA row hands the model a question, supplied source
knowledge, and a \emph{supplied candidate answer}, and reads the internal geometry at the single
token where the model commits to a YES/NO faithfulness judgment. This is \emph{recognition} of a
presented candidate at verification time---not detection of the model's \emph{own} hallucinations
during free generation. No result here shows these signals flag a model inventing a false claim
mid-paragraph.
\item \textbf{Stimuli.} The hallucinated candidates are HaluEval's dataset-synthesized
counterfeits. Stylistic or generator-specific shortcuts cannot be excluded in principle; the
paired faithful/hallucinated design controls the question, not the counterfeit's provenance.
\item \textbf{Inference.} A1 carries stem-clustered OOB confidence intervals; A2 is a
pre-registered point estimate with no interval, and all ten A2 folds share the same $1000$ items
per model (the holdout is over models, not items or distributions).
\item \textbf{Cohort.} Ten purposively chosen open-weight models, $1.7$B--$8$B, all 4-bit MLX on
Apple silicon. ``Cohort'' is the load-bearing word in every universality statement above.
\end{itemize}

\section{Provenance and honest attestation}

The extension is published with an enforced checksum chain rather than a trust-me summary. A sidecar
manifest (\texttt{PROVENANCE.json}, schema \texttt{bench-provenance/1.1}) binds a SHA-256 for every
one of the $53$ score matrices and $53$ profiles, the run sidecars, and the lifecycle logs, together
with the expected counts and matrix-to-profile pairing. A verifier
(\texttt{verify\_bench\_provenance.py}, exercised in CI) recomputes the tree and fails closed on any
mismatch, unsafe path, or malformed manifest. We are deliberate about its scope: it establishes
\emph{repository coherence}---that the published files match their declared checksums, counts, and
matrix-to-profile pairing---not an execution-time signature, and it recomputes no endpoint. Where a
re-run was used to re-attest the closed result, it is recorded under
its own dated name; the original detached run's exit status was never captured and is documented as
unrecoverable rather than reconstructed after the fact.

\paragraph{Comparability.} All HaluEval-QA cells are reported within a single MLX extraction stack and
are byte-comparable with one another. They are \emph{not} pooled with the study's non-byte-comparable
extension cells (GPU/torch scale panels or reimplemented multimodal extractors); those live in
separate, clearly labeled sections of the parent study.

\section{A note on label cost}

The sealed study's label-efficiency analysis (E3) is measured on the sealed ANLI/TriviaQA
cells---\emph{not} on HaluEval-QA---and only at budgets $n\in\{50,100,150\}$. Mean geometric
deployability rises $0.44\!\rightarrow\!0.66\!\rightarrow\!0.79$ across those budgets and is
\emph{still rising} at the largest one, so on those tasks $150$ labels is a measured lower bound,
not a sufficiency threshold or a ``knee.'' A1 here calibrates on $1000$ rows ($500$ stems) per
model and therefore supplies \emph{no} HaluEval-QA label-cost estimate: per-model calibration is
what makes this task deployable, calibration is not free, and how few labels would suffice to fix
a model's orientation on it is untested.

\section{Forward work (drafted, not filed or run)}
\label{sec:forward}

Two forward-work documents have been drafted; neither is filed as a registration, executed, nor
applied retroactively to the closed run. The first proposes a future pre-registration with a
per-cell commitment error budget and a widened acceptable-answer template with an explicit
blip-versus-behavior split, to be filed blind before any fresh data is inspected. The second
specifies a designed-retrospective screen---predictor frozen in advance---for a suggestive
coincidence: three of the four models whose orientation inverts here overlap a three-model
partial-transfer cluster seen in an earlier sealed morphology run. That overlap is logged as an
observation, not a finding, and any confirmatory claim is deferred to a separately filed design
and fresh-data replication.

\paragraph{Bottom line.} On a third, independent hallucination family, the registered verdict is
that the floor \emph{partially extends}: calibration holds ($10/10$) and the one pre-registered
fixed orientation does not transfer ($6/10$, the named failing endpoint), failing by inversion
rather than by noise. The replication bar is missed ($7/20$, a commitment-protocol cascade over
descriptively intact geometry), and both sealed orphans pass their registered re-test probe. The
signal is real on all ten models; what failed is the one label-free way this run pre-registered
to ship it.
