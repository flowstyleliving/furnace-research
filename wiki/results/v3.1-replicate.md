## 🎯 Major update — J_n correction (2026-04-25): the geometry was wrong, the picture is more nuanced

We discovered the pre-norm pipeline was missing the RMSNorm Jacobian `J_n` in its Fisher pullback. The sealed E17b at rank=1 plane projects pre-norm `Δh` onto a basis derived from `sqrt(p_t)·W_u` (which lives in **post-norm** `h`-space). The correction: project `J_n(h_prev) · Δh_pre` instead — the linear approximation of `Δh_post` that the Fisher pullback formula assumes.

Cross-model **Δ(F−R) AUROC at sealed rank=1 plane, 2000-sample bootstrap, three Δh choices, N=100**:

### Mistral's raw_top1 is not what we thought

Token analysis on Mistral's static W_u top-1 right singular vector reveals it's dominated by **code-domain tokens** (`ICENSE`, `qpoint`, `ityEngine`, `ERCHANTABILITY`, `<s>`, `>\r`) — not YES/NO/answer tokens. Yet under J_n geometry, raw_top1 discriminates contradictions at 0.99 AUROC.

Mechanism (from per-sample signed projections at gen_step=1, N=100):
- 100% of samples (both control AND contradiction) project POSITIVELY onto raw_top1
- Mean signed projection: ctrl `+3.01`, contr `+4.64`, Δ `+1.63`
- raw_top1 is a **magnitude axis** — both YES and NO predictions activate it; contradictions activate it more strongly
- Both Mistral controls AND contradictions emit `'\n'` as the first generated token (gen_step=1) — Mistral writes a newline before the answer
- The discriminator is "how strongly Mistral commits to start-the-answer" rather than "which answer" — raw_top1 captures this magnitude

Cross-model gen_step=1 token differs:
- 🦙 Llama: typically `'\n'`/`' Answer'`-style (similar to Mistral)
- 🌀 Mistral: 100% `'\n'` — answer not yet committed
- 🐉 Qwen 2.5: front-loads `' Answer'` / `'YES'` / `'NO'`
- 🐲 Qwen 3: varied — `' Answer'` / `' Let'` / `' Alright'` (CoT-style preamble or front-loaded)

**This means our sealed gen_step=1 plane captures different semantic moments per model** — for Mistral/Llama it's "begin the answer block," for Qwens it's actual answer content. Sealed E18 is unaffected (residualization washes this out). Sealed E17b, which depends on the geometric structure of `Δh`, IS affected — different models' `Δh` at gen_step=1 represent qualitatively different commitments.

### Refined paper position

The original v3 hypothesis "Fisher pullback uniformly beats static raw subspace" is **partially supported under proper geometry**:
- ✅ **Qwen 3**: Fisher CRUSHES raw at sealed rank=1 (Δ=+0.21 with non-overlap CI)
- 🟡 **Qwen 2.5**: Fisher slightly wins at rank=1 (Δ=+0.015, CI crosses 0); robustly wins at rank ≥ 4
- 🟡 **Llama 3B**: indeterminate at N=100
- ❌ **Mistral 7B**: raw decisively wins at rank=1 — Mistral's W_u top-1 is "rupture-magnitude axis" that activates strongly enough to make Fisher's reweighting redundant

The cross-model picture is itself the headline scientific finding: **Fisher pullback's edge over static raw subspace depends on the model's gen_step=1 commitment structure**. Where the model commits to actual answer content (Qwen-family), Fisher reweighting captures additional discriminative information beyond W_u's static SVD spectrum. Where the model commits to formatting first ("write a newline"), the static W_u top-1 already aligns with the rupture-magnitude direction and Fisher reweighting can't add.

The pre-registered E17b at rank=1 was inherited from E18's rank-1 pin (which made sense for residualized magnitude-independence) but was never well-motivated for E17b's head-to-head metric. Pinning rank=1 happened to land on Qwen-family Fisher's WORST rank specifically. Under J_n at higher ranks (4-13), Fisher robustly beats raw on both Qwen models.

This is **paper-shapable** — v3 has scientific content but it's not "Fisher uniformly wins." It's "Fisher's edge depends on model architecture and the rank chosen for evaluation." More interesting than the original headline.

### Methodological note: orientation-agnostic vs directed Δ

A first pass of the analyzer used **directed** Δ AUROC = AUROC(F) − AUROC(R) and produced apparent N=100→N=200 sign flips for Mistral (−0.184 → +0.156) and Qwen3 (+0.206 → −0.253) — looked like a methodological earthquake. This was **wrong**: the original N=100 table in `wiki/log.md` (and the overnight summary that produced it) uses orientation-agnostic Δ via `scripts/overnight_summary.py:48-74`, which flips each AUROC to `max(AUROC, 1−AUROC)`. The flip treats basis sign as a free parameter — defensible because SVD top-r directions have arbitrary sign. With the matching convention, all four sealed-r=1 verdicts replicate (table above). Both conventions are computed in `scripts/diagnostics/analyze_stage2_n200.py` for transparency.

A note for the paper: the "Mistral wants raw" verdict at sealed r=1 is **specifically about discriminative power, not direction-of-discrimination**. AUROC(F)=0.158 and AUROC(R)=0.002 — both far below 0.5 — meaning higher null_ratio at rank=1 is *associated with non-contradiction* on Mistral, the *opposite* of the rupture hypothesis at this specific (model × rank). Raw "wins" in the sense that its inverted-direction discrimination is sharper than Fisher's. At Mistral's best rank=32, AUROC(F)=0.964 and AUROC(R)=0.530 — both above 0.5 and Fisher direction matches the hypothesis. The orientation-agnostic metric blurs this; the directed metric makes it visible. Probably worth showing both in the paper.

### Pre-data Gemma γ extraction bug — caught and fixed before any Gemma main-run data was captured

Gemma 3 RMSNorm uses the "+1" formulation (`mx.fast.rms_norm(x, 1.0 + self.weight, eps)` in `mlx_lm/gemma3_text.py:110-111`); the pipeline's `_extract_final_rmsnorm_gamma` originally returned the raw `.weight` for all families, which on Gemma would have multiplied Δh_post by ≈0 (or a sign-flipped near-zero vector) instead of by `1 + weight` — silently zeroing every `null_ratio_*_post_rank{r}` column on Gemma alone. Detected, patched (Gemma-3-only branch keyed on `core.sliding_window_pattern`), and verified end-to-end before any Gemma main-run data landed. A second precision sub-bug surfaced on Gemma 3-4B (bf16-stored weight): adding 1.0 in fp32 after casting introduced \~0.4% per-channel rounding compounding to 3.6% max-abs error vs the model's own forward; resolved by performing the `1 + weight` operation at the weight's native dtype before casting to fp32. Verified: extracted γ reproduces `model.model.norm(h)` to ≤1e-5 max-abs error across all 6 families via `scripts/verify_gamma_extraction.py`. Other 4 primaries unaffected (their RMSNorm applies `weight` directly).

## Chain-length stratification — 2-of-6 sealed-r=1 verdicts are Simpson's-paradox-like (preliminary, n=100/stratum)

The puzzle generator is a 2×2 factorial (chain_length ∈ {2, 5} × contradiction ∈ {F, T}). Pooled n=200 mixes both chain lengths. Stratifying by chain_length on the existing 2026-04-26 J_n-corrected data (n=100/stratum, 50 ctrl × 50 contr) reveals that the cross-architecture verdict at sealed r=1 is **stable for 4 models and chain-length-dependent for 2** — Llama and Mistral have pooled verdicts that don't survive disaggregation:

**Mistral is the cleanest Simpson's-paradox case:** pooled n=200 reads Δ=−0.153 [−0.214, −0.101] sign Raw-decisive, but at *both* chain-length strata the Fisher metric is at-or-above Raw (cl=2: +0.035 barely Fisher, cl=5: −0.006 tied). The "Raw decisive" verdict at the pooled level is an artifact of mixing two chain-length subgroups whose Fisher and Raw discrimination axes have different orientations relative to contradiction.

**Largest cross-stratum spread:** Mistral 7B at rank=32, where cl=2 reads Δ=−0.238 (Raw decisive) and cl=5 reads Δ=+0.284 (Fisher decisive) — a cross-stratum delta of **−0.522**. Mistral's architecture-dependence is rank × chain-length **interactive**, not just a per-rank flip.

**General pattern across all 6 models and 4 sampled ranks (1, 13, 32, 64):** |Δ| is larger at cl=2 than cl=5. Short reasoning chains produce sharper Fisher-vs-Raw discrimination; long chains diffuse the rupture signal. This is consistent with the v3 hypothesis that gen_step=1 is the "commit moment" — in a 2-step chain the commit is closer to the contradiction; in a 5-step chain, intermediate reasoning tokens dilute the gen_step=1 commit content.

**n=100/stratum is borderline-powered for the +0.02 sealed bar** — Hanley-McNeil SE on Δ AUROC at this size is \~0.07, so non-overlap CIs require Δ ≥ 0.10. The Llama and Mistral FLIP cases sit in the borderline zone. A fresh n=150/cell sweep is in flight (background launch 2026-04-26, \~14h ETA) — once it lands, the same stratification at n=300/stratum (Hanley-McNeil SE \~0.04) will discriminate Simpson's-paradox-flip from finite-sample-noise.
